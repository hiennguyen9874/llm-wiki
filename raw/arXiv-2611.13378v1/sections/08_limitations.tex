\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

While our production serving benchmarks evaluate a frontier MoE architecture (\vfour-Flash, with a 552B-parameter backbone), our controlled pretraining quality ablations are conducted on dense models up to 340M parameters within a \val{cfg/seq}-token context. Pretraining a frontier-scale looped \ced MoE end-to-end—potentially incorporating lightweight post-training alignment~\citep{benice} to guarantee fixed-point convergence at extreme scale—and evaluating on broader long-context reasoning benchmarks remain important next steps.

Our serving results measure cost, not quality: the looped \vfour-Flash runs with dummy or original weights that were not trained to loop, so lossless last-round KV at this scale is assumed from our smaller models (\S\ref{sec:res-kv}). Per-round KV is emulated by capping the KV pool, and every run uses a single GB200 tray or H200 node rather than disaggregated or large expert-parallel deployments. Finally, because the encoder loop runs every round over the prompt, it costs more per request than a decoder loop on prompt-heavy workloads (Appendix~\ref{app:serving-more}), and this premium grows with larger decode batches.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work introduces \ourmethod and establishes how to combine cross-layer KV consolidation (\ced) with recurrent-depth computation (Looped Transformers) so that looping deeper does not inflate KV memory. \textit{First}, resolving where to loop, we show that recurrence must be placed in the \ced encoder so that each round actively refines a global KV source, matching looped dense Transformers in validation BPB and long-document scaling while avoiding the long-range degradation of decoder or local-window loops. \textit{Second}, resolving how to loop, we demonstrate that training with stochastic recurrence depth drives the recurrent encoder toward a contractive fixed point, enabling lossless cross-round KV sharing with zero additional KV memory over an unlooped model. \textit{Third}, in production serving on $4\times\text{GB200}$ and $8\times\text{H200}$ nodes with \vfour-Flash, these dual savings shrink resident KV memory by $36.8\times$ over loops with per-layer and per-round KV ($5.2\times$ from cross-round sharing alone at $T=8$), delivering $3.1\times$--$16.5\times$ higher throughput across standard and 128K long-context workloads while cutting multi-turn agent TTFT by $13.6\times$--$29.7\times$ against per-round KV.
