\begin{abstract}

Unsupervised large-scale vision-language pre-training has shown promising advances on various downstream tasks. Existing methods often  model the cross-modal interaction either via the similarity of the global feature of each modality which misses sufficient information, or finer-grained interactions using cross/self-attention upon visual  and textual tokens. However, cross/self-attention suffers from inferior efficiency in both training and inference. In this paper, we introduce a large-scale Fine-grained Interactive Language-Image Pre-training (FILIP) to achieve finer-level alignment through a cross-modal late interaction mechanism, which uses a token-wise maximum similarity between visual and textual tokens to guide the contrastive objective. FILIP successfully leverages the finer-grained expressiveness between image patches and textual words by modifying only contrastive loss, while simultaneously gaining the ability to pre-compute image and text representations offline at inference, keeping both large-scale training and inference efficient. Furthermore, we construct a new large-scale image-text pair dataset called FILIP300M for pre-training. Experiments show that FILIP achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple downstream vision-language tasks including zero-shot image classification and image-text retrieval. The visualization on word-patch alignment further shows that FILIP can learn meaningful fine-grained features with promising localization ability.

However, cross/self-attention suffers from inferior efficiency in terms of both training and inference. In this paper, we introduce a large-scale Fine-grained Interacitve Language-Image Pretraining (FILIP) to achieve finer-level alignment through a new cross-modal late interaction mechanism, which can boost the performance on more grounded vision and language tasks. Our fine-grained contrastive learning uses a token-wise maximum similarity to guide the contrastive objective between patches in the images and words in the sentences while keeping the large-scale training and inference efficient. To support more down-stream generation tasks, FILIP further incorporates a light interaction layer on the top of the original dual-encoder architecture. FILIP can successfully leverage the finer-grained expressiveness while simultaneously gaining the ability to pre-compute image and text representations offline. Furthermore, we also present a new large-scale FILIP350M pretraining dataset for facilitating research community. Extensive experiments show FILIP achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple downstream vision-language tasks including Image-text Retrieval, and zero-shot image classification. The visualization further shows that FILIP can learn meaningful finer-grained features with promising localization ability. On zero-shot classification and image-text retrieval, our FILIP outperforms current state-of-the-art visual-language pretraining methods (e.g. CLIP, ALIGN) in a large margin.

Other methods use cross-attention or self-attention to capture finer-grained interactions, but require more complex model structure or are less efficient in computation. In this work, we propose a  late interaction loss  to model the token-level alignment between the two modalities,  which maintain the dual-stream model's ability to pre-compute image and text representations offline for efficient inference, yet retain the fine-grained interaction between text and image. To further explore finer-grained expressiveness and support generation task, we also add an interaction layer on top of the dual-stream model.
