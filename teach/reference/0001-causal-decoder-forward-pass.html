Causal decoder forward pass

Use this when an architecture paper introduces a component and you need to ask: where does it sit in the baseline path?

1 · Token IDs
[T] discrete vocabulary indexes from a subword tokenizer.
↓
2 · Token + position representations
[T, d] continuous vectors; position information prevents order from being lost.
↓
3 · Repeated decoder blocks
causal self-attention mixes permitted earlier positions; an MLP transforms each position; residual and normalization paths carry the representation across depth.
↓
4 · Hidden states
[T, d] one contextual representation at each position.
↓
5 · Vocabulary projection → logits
[T, V] one unnormalized score per vocabulary item for each position.
↓
6 · Softmax → next-token distribution
[T, V] normalized probabilities. A generation policy selects the next token, which is decoded to text.

Two invariants

  • Causality: for position i, attention blocks positions after i. This is what permits next-token prediction without future-token leakage.
  • Shapes: attention/MLP blocks preserve the model width d; only the final vocabulary projection changes it to V.
Paper-reading prompt. When you meet a new mechanism, label it first as a change to token/position representation, sequence mixing, per-token transformation, residual/normalization, vocabulary output, or inference state. Then ask what cost or limitation it targets.

Sources and boundary

The original Transformer paper documents attention, position-wise feed-forward layers, residual/normalization paths, and positional encodings. Radford et al.’s GPT paper documents the decoder-only causal language-model adaptation and BPE vocabulary. The original Transformer is encoder–decoder; this reference uses its decoder-side components as the dense causal baseline.
