Trace a causal decoder

Your first paper-reading skill: turn “decoder-only Transformer” into a concrete flow of representations and shapes.

Mission connection: architecture papers are much easier to read when you can locate a proposed change in the baseline forward pass—then ask which cost, capability, or deployment constraint it changes.

The one idea

An LLM does not directly manipulate words. A subword tokenizer first maps text to vocabulary IDs. A GPT-style model maps those IDs to vectors, repeatedly contextualizes them with causal decoder blocks, and converts the final vectors to vocabulary scores. Softmax turns those scores (logits) into a distribution for the next token.

Text → token IDs [T]
↓
Token + position representations [T, d]
↓
N × causal decoder blocks [T, d]
↓
Vocabulary projection → logits [T, V]
↓
Softmax → next-token probabilities [T, V]

T is the number of tokens already in context, d the model width, and V the vocabulary size. The crucial architectural observation is that decoder blocks preserve d; the final projection is where the model becomes a score over every vocabulary entry.

Why “causal” matters

At position i, self-attention is masked so it cannot use positions after i. Thus the representation at each position is built only from permitted preceding context. This masking, not merely the word “GPT,” makes the decoder suitable for autoregressive next-token prediction.

Don’t overgeneralize. The original Transformer paper describes an encoder–decoder architecture. GPT adapts the masked decoder side into a decoder-only language model. Keep those two baselines distinct when reading papers.

Fast retrieval check

Which matrix has shape [T, V] immediately before softmax?

Use it on a paper

For every claimed architectural novelty, write one sentence in this template:

“It changes [baseline stage] to address [bottleneck], trading [cost or capability] for [cost or risk].”

Example—not a claim about any particular paper: “It changes sequence mixing to reduce inference state, trading token-addressable retrieval for bounded memory.” This will become your lens for attention variants, KV-cache designs, MoE, and recurrent alternatives.

Read next

Read only the architecture section, then identify the attention, per-position transformation, residual/normalization, and positional-information stages.
