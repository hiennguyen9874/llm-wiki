# Money, income, saving, and financial wealth — transactions, stocks, and flows

**Money** is the financial assets usable for transactions (currency and checkable deposits); it is neither a synonym for **income** nor for **financial wealth**. Income and saving are flows per unit of time, whereas financial wealth is a point-in-time stock of financial assets minus financial liabilities.[^blanchard-semantic-traps]

## The distinctions

| Term | Meaning | Stock or flow |
|---|---|---|
| Money | Currency and checkable deposits used to pay for transactions | Stock |
| Income | Earnings from work plus interest and dividends received | Flow |
| Saving | After-tax income not spent | Flow |
| Financial wealth | Value of financial assets less financial liabilities | Stock |

A person's wealth changes over time through saving or dissaving and through changes in asset and liability values. At a moment in time, changing its composition does not change its total: using a checking-account balance to repay a mortgage reduces both an asset and a liability by the same amount.[^blanchard-semantic-traps]

## Portfolio allocation is not saving

Given wealth, choosing how much to hold as money rather than bonds is a portfolio decision, not a decision about how much to save. Money is transaction-convenient but pays no interest in the simplified model; bonds pay interest but entail conversion costs when funds are needed. Desired money holdings therefore rise with transactions and fall as the bond interest rate makes the forgone return from money larger.[^blanchard-4-1]

In macroeconomic terminology, **investment** means purchasing newly produced capital goods such as machines, plants, or office buildings. Purchases of shares or other financial assets are instead *financial investment*; they are not investment in this narrower accounting sense.[^blanchard-semantic-traps]
