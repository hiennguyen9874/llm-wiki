Synthesis: real-time detection is converging from `YOLO = dense + NMS` versus `DETR = sparse queries + bipartite matching` toward NMS-free end-to-end inference, with dense supervision used only during training and refined localization modeling[^reseach-2026-09-17-1].

## Dense training, sparse inference

- **Reported:** RT-DETRv3 (WACV 2025) adds hierarchical dense positive supervision to fix sparse Hungarian one-to-one supervision, keeping inference end-to-end[^reseach-2026-09-17-2].
- **Reported:** DEIM (CVPR 2025) introduces Dense one-to-one matching plus Matchability-Aware Loss; paper reports ~50% training-time reduction in its experiments[^reseach-2026-09-17-3][^deim-2412-04234-v3-1].
- **Reported:** YOLO26 provides native one-to-one NMS-free head, drops DFL, and uses Progressive Loss plus Small-Target-Aware Label Assignment (STAL); analysis-paper evidence is compiled in the YOLO26 section below[^yolo26-2509-25164-v5-2][^yolo26-2509-25164-v5-3][^reseach-2026-09-17-4].
- **Synthesis:** the durable pattern is **train dense → infer sparse**: rich positive signals while learning, simple NMS-free pipeline at deployment.

## Localization as distribution refinement

- **Reported:** identity is `D-FINE: Redefine Regression Task in DETRs as Fine-grained Distribution Refinement` by Peng, Li, Wu, Zhang, Sun, and Wu (USTC), ICLR 2025 style, arXiv `2410.13842v1`; problem is Dirac-delta fixed-coordinate regression cannot model localization uncertainty and forces coarse L1/IoU guidance, while GFocal-style distributions remain anchor-dependent, coarse, and one-shot without iterative refinement[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-1].
- **Reported:** Fine-grained Distribution Refinement (FDR) makes distributions the iterative intermediate: first decoder layer predicts initial box `b0={x,y,W,H}` plus preliminary distributions via separate MLP heads, later layers add residual logits `Pr^l=Softmax(logits^{l-1}+Δlogits^l)` and compute edge distances `d^l=d0+{H,H,W,W}·ΣW(n)Pr^l(n)` scaled by initial box size[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-2].
- **Reported:** non-uniform weighting `W(n)` with `a=1/2`, `c=1/4` gives small curvature near accurate predictions for fine adjustment and large edge curvature plus sharp `±2a` boundaries for large corrections; bins `N=32`; Fine-Grained Localization loss is IoU-weighted interpolation CE against the two bins adjacent to relative offset `φ=(dGT−d0)/{H,H,W,W}`, concentrating low-uncertainty distributions[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-2].
- **Reported:** Global Optimal Localization Self-Distillation (GO-LSD) uses the final decoder layer as its own localization teacher with no extra teacher or inference cost; per-layer Hungarian matches are unioned into a global set plus unmatched predictions, while classification stays one-to-one so no duplicate boxes emerge; Decoupled Distillation Focal loss is temperature-smoothed KL (`T=5`) with matched weight `α=IoU·√Km/(√Km+√Ku)` and unmatched weight `β=Conf·√Ku/(√Km+√Ku)`[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-3].
- **Synthesis:** the durable mechanism is **bidirectional residual simplification** — shallow layers learn from refined final distributions, so deeper layers need only refine smaller residuals.
- **Reported:** lightweight path from RT-DETR-HGNetv2-L baseline is remove decoder projection/value/output layers, add Target Gating after cross-attention replacing the residual, replace encoder CSP with half-hidden-dimension GELAN, uneven deformable sampling `(S:3,M:6,L:3)`, and RT-DETRv2 training strategy; roadmap goes 53.0% AP / 32M / 110 GFLOPs / 9.25 ms to **54.0% / 31M / 91 GFLOPs / 8.07 ms** (−13% latency, −17% GFLOPs), with FDR +0.5 and GO-LSD +0.5 in that chain[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-4].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017`, T4 TensorRT FP16: **D-FINE-L 54.0/71.6/58.4** (31M, 91 GFLOPs, 8.07 ms) and **D-FINE-X 55.8/73.7/60.2** (62M, 202 GFLOPs, 12.89 ms), above YOLOv10-L 53.2, YOLOv10-X 54.4, RT-DETR-R50 53.1, R101 54.3, HG-L 53.0, HG-X 54.8, RT-DETRv2-L 53.4/X 54.3, and LW-DETR-X 53.0 at lower or comparable cost; Objects365 pretraining for only 21 epochs (versus reported YOLOv10 300) reaches **L 57.1 / X 59.3**, +3.1/+4.4 over pretreated YOLOv10-L/X[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-5].
- **Reported:** same protocol gives S 48.5 (10.2M, 25.2 GFLOPs, 3.49 ms) and M 52.3 (19.2M, 56.6 GFLOPs, 5.55 ms), rising to 50.7/55.1 after Objects365; plug-and-play FDR plus GO-LSD at unchanged params gives Deformable-DETR 43.7→47.1 (+3.4), DAB-DETR 44.2→49.5 (+5.3), DN-DETR 46.0→49.7 (+3.7), DINO 49.0→51.6 (+2.6) at 12 epochs and 50.4→52.4 (+2.0) at 24 epochs[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-6].
- **Reported:** ablations peak at `a,c=1/2,1/4` (53.3; learnable 53.1; near-linear large-`c` 53.0), `N=32` (53.7; 64/128 plateau 53.6), and `T=5` (54.0); without lightweight changes, baseline 53.0/FDR 53.8/vanilla-LD 53.7/GO-LSD **54.5** with ~+6% time and +2% memory per epoch (29→31 min, 8552→8734 MB on 4×RTX 4090), while logit mimicking 52.6 and feature imitation 52.9 do not help DETR one-to-one matching[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-7].
- **Reported:** limitation is small-model gains stay narrow because shallow-decoder final predictions are weaker teachers; proposed future work trains extra sophisticated decoder layers and discards them at inference after GO-LSD transfers their localization knowledge[^dfine-2410-13842-v1-7].
- **Synthesis:** this extends the older shift in classification toward distributional or soft targets into localization as structured prediction.

## RT-DETRv2 bag-of-freebies baseline

- **Reported:** RT-DETRv2 (technical report, Baidu plus PKU Shenzhen) keeps the RT-DETR framework and changes only decoder deformable attention plus training; stated goals are flexibility, practicality, and better performance without speed loss[^rtdetrv2-2407-17140-v1-1].
- **Reported:** decoder uses a distinct number of sampling points per scale instead of the same count at every scale, for selective multi-scale feature extraction that respects intrinsic scale differences[^rtdetrv2-2407-17140-v1-1].
- **Reported:** optional `discrete_sample` replaces DETR-specific `grid_sample` by rounding predicted offsets and dropping bilinear interpolation; offset-prediction gradients are turned off, training first uses `grid_sample` for `6x` then fine-tunes `1x` with `discrete_sample`, and inference/deployment uses `discrete_sample`[^rtdetrv2-2407-17140-v1-2].
- **Reported:** dynamic augmentation keeps RT-DETR augmentation early then turns off `RandomPhotometricDistort`, `RandomZoomOut`, `RandomIoUCrop`, and `MultiScaleInput` in the last two epochs; scale-adaptive customization fixes detector LR at `1e-4` while backbone LR is S/R18 `1e-4`, M/R34 `5e-5`, L/R50 `1e-5`, X/R101 `1e-6`, on the rationale that lighter pretrained backbones need higher LR and larger ones need lower LR[^rtdetrv2-2407-17140-v1-3].
- **Reported:** implementation is ImageNet-pretrained ResNet with AdamW batch 16 and EMA `0.9999`; evaluation is COCO `train2017` / `val2017` with AP and AP50[^rtdetrv2-2407-17140-v1-4].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017` at 640×640, T4 TensorRT FP16 batch 1, same params/FPS as RT-DETR: S 47.9 (+1.4) / 64.9 (+1.1) at 217 FPS, M 49.9 (+1.0) / 67.5 (+0.7) at 161 FPS, M\* 51.9 (+0.6) / 69.9 (+0.3) at 145 FPS, L 53.4 (+0.3) / 71.6 (+0.3) at 108 FPS, X 54.3 (+0.0) / 72.8 (+0.1) at 74 FPS[^rtdetrv2-2407-17140-v1-5].
- **Reported:** sampling-point ablation on S (`num_head × num_point × num_query × num_decoder`): 86,400 → 47.9/64.9, 64,800 → 47.8/64.8, 43,200 → 47.7/64.7, 21,600 → 47.3/64.3; paper reads this as reducible sampling cost without major degradation[^rtdetrv2-2407-17140-v1-6].
- **Reported:** `discrete_sample` ablation keeps AP50 close to `grid_sample`: S 47.4/64.8 (−0.1 AP50), M 49.2/67.1 (−0.4), M\* 51.4/69.7 (−0.2), L 52.9/71.3 (−0.3); paper claims deployment constraints removed at small AP cost[^rtdetrv2-2407-17140-v1-7].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is scale-selective deformable sampling, integerized sampling for deployment-friendly DETR inference, late-schedule augmentation cooldown, and per-scale backbone-LR tuning; gains shrink with model scale and vanish at X, so treat v2 as a small-model efficiency/practicality baseline rather than a large-model accuracy jump.

## RT-DETRv3 hierarchical dense supervision

- **Reported:** identity is `RT-DETRv3: Real-time End-to-End Object Detection with Hierarchical Dense Positive Supervision` by Wang, Xia, Lv, and Shi (Baidu Inc, WACV 2025 Paper ID 1755), arXiv `2409.08475v3`; problem is sparse Hungarian one-to-one supervision undertraining both encoder and decoder relative to dense YOLO-style supervision[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-1].
- **Reported:** framework retains RT-DETR: ResNet backbone plus efficient hybrid encoder producing `C3/C4/C5`, uncertainty-minimal query selection, transformer decoder with self-attention, cross-attention, FFN, and MLP prediction; training loss uses L1 plus GIoU for boxes and VFL for classification[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-1].
- **Reported:** hierarchical dense supervision is training-only, so inference latency and params match RT-DETR/RT-DETRv2; three branches are `O2M-C` for the encoder, multi-group self-attention perturbation `MGSA` for decoder one-to-one diversity, and `O2M-T` shared-weight one-to-many decoder branch[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-1].
- **Reported:** `O2M-C` feeds encoder `C3/C4/C5` directly into a PP-YOLOE head with ATSS early then TaskAlign matching and VFL plus DFL losses; VFL uses IoU as the positive target so high-IoU samples dominate, consistent with the decoder head; branch loss is `L_aux` with weight `α`[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-2].
- **Reported:** `MGSA` creates `N` query sets `OQ_i` with random masks `M_i`, computes `Q_i,K_i,V_i=Linear(OQ_i)`, `W_i=Softmax(M_i(Q_i K_i^T))`, and `Ṽ_i=W_i V_i`; concatenated sets share one decoder, keep Hungarian matching and RT-DETR loss per set, and average as `L_o2o=(1/N)ΣL^i_o2o` with weight `β`; default is `N=3`[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-3].
- **Reported:** `O2M-T` adds one shared-weight decoder branch that replicates each ground truth `m=4` times against 300 queries to keep more high-quality queries per target; loss is `L_o2m` with weight `γ`; total is `L=αL_aux+βL_o2o+γL_o2m` with defaults `α=β=γ=1`[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-4].
- **Reported:** implementation follows RT-DETR augmentation and pretraining with AdamW weight decay `0.0001`, batch 64 on 4×A100, PP-YOLOE settings for the CNN branch, 3 perturbation groups, `m=4`, 300 queries, `10x`/120 epochs for R18/R34 and `6x`/72 epochs for R50/R101; latency is T4 TensorRT FP16 following RT-DETR[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-5].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017` RT-DETR-series table at 1x/3x/6x (12/36/72 epochs; `†`=120 epochs) with unchanged params/FLOPs/latency — R18 20M/60GFLOPs/4.6ms: 41.5/46.1/48.1/48.7†; R34 31M/92GFLOPs/6.3ms: 44.7/48.6/49.9/50.1†; R50 42M/136GFLOPs/9.2ms: 50.2/53.0/53.4; R101 76M/259GFLOPs/13.5ms: 51.3/54.2/54.6[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-6].
- **Reported:** at 6x, gains over RT-DETR are R18 +1.6, R34 +1.0, R50 +0.3, R101 +0.3; over RT-DETRv2 on R18/R34 at 6x/10x are +1.4/+0.8 and +0.9/+0.2; paper claims about half the epochs for comparable accuracy and ≤60% of CNN epochs at higher AP[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-6].
- **Reported:** CNN comparison at COCO `val2017`: R18 48.7 beats YOLOv6-3.0-S/Gold-YOLO-S/YOLO-MS-S/YOLOv8-S/YOLOv9-S/YOLOv10-S by 4.4/3.3/2.5/2.5/2.0/2.4; R34 50.1 and R50m 51.7 (36M/100GFLOPs/6.89ms) lead medium baselines; R50 53.4 and R101 54.6 beat YOLOv10-L 53.2 and YOLOv10-X 54.4; paper notes the framework is not yet lightweight-optimized[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-7].
- **Reported:** large backbones overfit on COCO-only: R50 53.4 at 51 epochs → 52.9 at 72 (−0.5) and R101 54.6 → 54.2 (−0.4); joint COCO plus Objects365 restores scaling to R50 54.2 → 54.8 (+0.6) and R101 54.7 → 55.4 (+0.7); paper attributes this to data/model-size mismatch plus faster convergence[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-8].
- **Reported:** R18 ablations from 46.5 baseline: +O2M-C 47.4, +O2M-T 47.5, +MGSA 47.5, all three 48.1 (+1.6); MGSA count 2/3/4 gives 47.9/65.4, 48.1/65.6, 48.0/65.3 AP/AP50, so 3 is optimal with more branches adding learning difficulty without gain[^rtdetrv3-2409-08475-v3-9].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is decoupled train-only density — CNN one-to-many head for encoder representation, masked multi-query groups to diversify one-to-one assignment, plus replicated-target one-to-many decoder loss — while preserving NMS-free inference; expect largest wins on small backbones and short schedules, and add data before extending large-backbone schedules.

## DEIM dense matching and matchability-aware loss

- **Reported:** identity is `DEIM: DETR with Improved Matching for Fast Convergence` by Huang, Lu, Cun, Yu, Zhou, and Shen (Intellindust AI Lab and others), arXiv `2412.04234v3`; framework combines Dense one-to-one matching with Matchability-Aware Loss (MAL) and is presented as a training-only change with no inference overhead[^deim-2412-04234-v3-1].
- **Reported:** diagnosis for slow DETR convergence is twofold: sparse supervision because Hungarian one-to-one gives one positive per target, and low-quality matches because 100–300 randomly initialized queries lack spatial alignment and cluster around salient objects[^deim-2412-04234-v3-2].
- **Reported:** measured on RT-DETRv2-R50 plus COCO, one-to-one peaks below 10 positives per image while SimOTA one-to-many spreads much wider and sometimes exceeds 80, reaching about 10× more matches in extreme cases[^deim-2412-04234-v3-3].
- **Reported:** Dense one-to-one keeps `M_i=1` in `loss=Σ_iΣ_j f(yhat_ij,y_i)` but raises target count `N` per image with mosaic plus mixup, e.g. stitching four quadrants to go from 1 target / 1 positive to 4 targets / 4 positives at unchanged image size; unlike Group DETR, H-DETR, or Co-DETR it needs no extra parallel decoders or auxiliary one-to-many heads[^deim-2412-04234-v3-2][^deim-2412-04234-v3-3].
- **Reported:** Varifocal Loss is `VFL=-q(q log p+(1-q) log(1-p))` for `q>0` and `-α p^γ log(1-p)` for `q=0` with `q` as IoU; paper argues it under-penalizes low-IoU matches and discards zero-overlap matches as negatives, which matters less for dense-anchor detectors than for sparse-query DETRs[^deim-2412-04234-v3-4].
- **Reported:** MAL is `MAL=-q^γ log p-(1-q^γ) log(1-p)` for `y=1` and `-p^γ log(1-p)` for `y=0`; it changes the positive target from `q` to `q^γ` and drops VFL `α`, penalizing low-quality matches more sharply while behaving like VFL on high-quality matches; reported `γ=1.5` is best at 24 epochs with 52.4 AP versus 52.2/52.1/51.9 at 1.3/1.8/2.0[^deim-2412-04234-v3-4].
- **Reported:** training uses mosaic plus mixup for Dense one-to-one, AdamW, color jitter plus zoom-out, ImageNet1k-pretrained backbones, COCO `train2017` / `val2017` at 640×640, FlatCosine LR plus a DataAug scheduler that warms up without advanced augmentation for about 4 epochs, disables Dense one-to-one after 50% of epochs, and turns off augmentation in the last 2 epochs[^deim-2412-04234-v3-5].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017` ResNet results: RT-DETRv2-R50 72-epoch baseline 53.4 AP goes to DEIM 53.9 at 36 epochs and 54.3 at 60 epochs; R101 baseline 54.3 goes to 55.2 at 36 and 55.5 at 60; small-object gains are R50 +1.3 and R101 +2.1 AP_S at comparable overall AP[^deim-2412-04234-v3-6].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017` real-time results at unchanged params/FLOPs/latency: DEIM-D-FINE-L 54.7 AP at 8.07 ms and DEIM-D-FINE-X 56.5 AP at 12.89 ms from 50 epochs versus D-FINE-L 54.0 and D-FINE-X 55.8 from 72 epochs; X small-object AP rises 37.3→38.8; DEIM-D-FINE-L beats reported YOLO11-X 54.1 AP at 10.52 ms with about 20% less latency[^deim-2412-04234-v3-6].
- **Reported:** ablations on RT-DETRv2-R50 and D-FINE-L show Dense one-to-one alone nearly matches the 72-epoch baseline in 36 epochs, while adding MAL further raises AP and especially AP75; mosaic plus mixup at 0.5/0.5 gives 52.5 AP at 24 epochs versus 51.7 without target augmentation, and average objects per image 10→25 helps while 50 hurts through positive-negative imbalance and distribution shift[^deim-2412-04234-v3-7][^deim-2412-04234-v3-8].
- **Reported:** training cost is almost unchanged per epoch at about 1.183 versus 1.181 GPU-hours on one 4090, but convergence needs about 71 versus 85 GPU-hours; paper also claims 53.2 AP in about one day on one 4090 with RT-DETRv2, Object365-pretrained D-FINE-X fine-tuning 59.3 at 32 epochs → 59.5 at 24 epochs with DEIM, and CrowdHuman D-FINE-L 56.0→57.5 AP with AP_S 29.0→33.2[^deim-2412-04234-v3-7].
- **Reported:** limits are residual small-object gap to YOLOv9-E despite higher overall AP, over-dense images degrading accuracy, and minor settings such as unfrozen backbone BN plus FlatCosine plus SiLU decoder contributing only about 0–0.1 AP; training AP slightly lower with higher val AP is read as reduced overfitting[^deim-2412-04234-v3-8].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is increase `N` rather than `M_i` for dense training supervision, plus IoU-sharpened classification loss for sparse-query low-quality matches; both are training-only and compose with RT-DETRv2 and D-FINE without changing NMS-free inference.

## DEIMv2 DINOv3 adaptation with STA, efficient decoder, and Copy-Blend

- **Reported:** identity is `Real-Time Object Detection Meets DINOv3` (DEIMv2) by Huang, Hou, Liu, Yu, and Shen (Intellindust AI Lab plus Xiamen University), arXiv `2509.20787v4`; it extends the DEIM pipeline with DINOv3 features, keeps RT-DETR backbone plus hybrid encoder plus decoder with top-K selection and iterative refinement, and spans eight sizes X/L/M/S plus Nano/Pico/Femto/Atto for GPU, edge, and mobile[^deimv2-2509-20787-v4-1].
- **Reported:** ViT variants keep 12 backbone layers and 3 scales with 300 queries: X uses ViT-S+ 384-dim and L uses ViT-S 384-dim from official DINOv3, M distills ViT-T+ 256-dim and S distills ViT-T 192-dim from ViT-Small DINOv3; encoder/decoder dims are 256 for X/L/M and 192 for S[^deimv2-2509-20787-v4-1].
- **Reported:** Spatial Tuning Adapter (STA) runs parallel to DINOv3 as a fully convolutional adapter with an ultra-lightweight detail branch plus Bi-Fusion `1x1` convolutions; it resizes single-scale 1/16 outputs from several ViT blocks (e.g. 5th, 8th, 11th) by parameter-free bilinear interpolation instead of ViTDet Feature2Pyramid deconvolution, while fast image downsampling supplies small-receptive-field multi-scale details to complement DINOv3 semantics[^deimv2-2509-20787-v4-2].
- **Reported:** efficient decoder keeps deformable attention but replaces FFN/LayerNorm with SwiGLU FFN and RMSNorm and shares one query position embedding across all decoder layers because query locations change minimally during refinement[^deimv2-2509-20787-v4-2].
- **Reported:** enhanced Dense O2O adds object-level Copy-Blend that inserts objects without backgrounds and blends rather than overwrites like Copy-Paste; total loss is `L=1.0*MAL+0.15*FGL+1.5*DDF+5*L1+2*GIoU`, with FGL plus DDF local loss kept for X/L/M/S/Nano and excluded for Pico/Femto/Atto because limited capacity weakens self-distillation[^deimv2-2509-20787-v4-3].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017` primary results are X 57.8 AP (50.26M, 151.6 GFLOPs) versus DEIM-X 56.5 (62M, 202 GFLOPs), L 56.0 (32.18M, 96.32 GFLOPs), M 53.0 (18.11M, 52.20 GFLOPs), S 50.9 (9.71M, 25.62 GFLOPs) as the first reported sub-10M model above 50 AP versus DEIM-S 49.0, and Nano 43.0 (3.57M, 6.86 GFLOPs); paper states latency is unoptimized and names FlashAttention as in YOLOv12 a future speedup[^deimv2-2509-20787-v4-4].
- **Reported:** gains concentrate on medium/large objects while small objects barely move: e.g. S goes 52.6 to 55.3 AP_M and 65.7 to 70.3 AP_L but only 30.4 to 31.4 AP_S versus DEIM-S, and X reaches 39.2 AP_S with 62.9 AP_M and 75.9 AP_L; paper reads this as DINOv3 supplying strong global semantics with limited fine-grained detail[^deimv2-2509-20787-v4-4].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is **foundation ViT plus parameter-free pyramidization plus detail injection plus training-only density** — adapt single-scale foundation features without costly upsampling, restore locality with a cheap image branch, and keep MAL plus FGL/DDF plus Copy-Blend supervision training-only so NMS-free inference is unchanged.

## LW-DETR as NMS-free ViT baseline

- **Reported:** architecture is plain ViT encoder plus C2f projector plus 3-layer deformable DETR decoder with mixed query selection; tiny uses 6 encoder layers, dim 192, 100 queries and single 1/16 scale, while small/medium use 10 layers and 300 queries and large/xlarge use two-scale 1/8 plus 1/32 projector outputs with multi-scale decoder[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-1].
- **Reported:** encoder aggregates intermediate plus final ViT feature maps for richer features; ablation on small at 12 epochs gives +0.7 mAP from 35.3 to 36.0[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-2].
- **Reported:** interleaved window/global attention cuts FLOPs from 23.0G to 16.6G but row-major ViTDet-style implementation raises latency 3.7 to 3.9 ms because of permutation; window-major organization removes the permutation and cuts latency to 2.9 ms at unchanged 34.7 mAP[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-3].
- **Reported:** training combines IoU-aware IA-BCE classification target `t=s^0.25·u^0.75` with `2.0·GIoU+5.0·L1`, Group DETR with 13 parallel weight-sharing decoders used only in training, Plain-DETR-style box reparameterization, CAEv2 MIM ViT pretraining on Objects365 (+0.7 mAP), then Objects365 detection pretraining for 30 epochs and COCO fine-tuning for 180K iterations with AdamW and EMA[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-4].
- **Reported:** 12-epoch small ablation chain is 35.3 initial, 36.0 multilevel, 34.7 windowed, 34.7 window-major, 35.4 IA loss, 38.4 Group supervision, 38.6 box reparameterization, 47.3 Objects365 pretraining (+8.7), 48.0 longer schedule[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-3].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017` at 640×640, T4 TensorRT 8.6.1 FP16 batch 1 end-to-end without NMS: tiny 42.6 AP / 2.0 ms, small 48.0 / 2.9 ms, medium 52.5 / 5.6 ms, large 56.1 / 8.8 ms, xlarge 58.3 / 19.1 ms[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-5].
- **Reported:** under the paper's shared protocol LW-DETR beats tuned YOLO/RTMDet/YOLO-NAS/YOLO-MS/Gold-YOLO plus RT-DETR and YOLOv10, e.g. small 48.0/2.9 ms versus YOLO-NAS-s 47.3/2.9 ms tuned and large 56.1/8.8 ms versus RT-DETR-R50 pretrained 55.3/9.3 ms and YOLOv10-X 54.4/10.70 ms[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-6].
- **Reported:** NMS accounting matters: default thresholds leave small-model latency NMS-dominated (YOLOv8n model 1.51 ms versus total 6.21 ms; RTMDet-t 2.16 versus 7.41); tuned thresholds cut NMS overhead to about 0.1–0.5 ms at −0.1 to −0.5 AP cost by reducing boxes fed to NMS, but LW-DETR still leads[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-7].
- **Reported:** Objects365 pretraining is asymmetric: LW-DETR gains mean +5.5 AP (tiny 36.5→42.6, small 43.6→48.0, medium 47.2→52.5, large 49.5→56.1, xlarge 53.0→58.3) and also helps R18/R50 convolutional LW-DETR variants, while YOLOv8/RTMDet/YOLO-MS/Gold-YOLO gains shrink as training epochs grow to 300–500[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-8].
- **Reported:** COCO-trained small generalizes: UVO cross-domain class-agnostic 32.3 mAP / 45.1 AP50 / 59.8 AR100 above RTMDet-s, YOLOv8-s and YOLO-NAS-s across small/medium/large recall; RF100 multi-domain fine-tuning averages 82.5 AP50 small and 83.5 medium above YOLOv8-s 80.1, RTMDet-s 79.2 and YOLO-NAS-s/m 81.5/81.8, with largest gaps in documents and electromagnetic domains[^lwdetr-2406-03459-v1-9].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is ViT-plus-shallow-decoder NMS-free design with multilevel aggregation, window-major interleaved attention, train-dense Group supervision plus IoU-aware target, and large-data detection pretraining; fair latency comparison must include tuned-threshold NMS time.

## YOLOv11 architectural efficiency baseline

- **Reported:** identity is `YOLOv11: An Overview of the Key Architectural Enhancements` (Khanam and Hussain, YOLO Vision 2024 context), arXiv `2410.17725v1`; it is a secondary overview extending the YOLOv8 backbone/neck with C3k2, SPPF, and C2PSA and spanning detection plus segmentation, pose, OBB, classification, and tracking[^yolo11-2410-17725-v1-1].
- **Reported:** backbone keeps YOLO-style downsampling convolutions but replaces the C2f block with C3k2, a CSP bottleneck using two smaller convolutions instead of one large convolution for faster processing with fewer parameters; `c3k=False` behaves like C2f while `c3k=True` uses a C3 block for deeper extraction, with a C3k variant allowing customizable kernel sizes[^yolo11-2410-17725-v1-2].
- **Reported:** SPPF is retained with a new C2PSA (CSP with Parallel Spatial Attention) block after it; neck replaces C2f with C3k2 after upsampling/concatenation; head uses multiple C3k2 blocks for multi-scale refinement followed by CBS (Convolution-BatchNorm-SiLU) layers and Conv2D plus Detect outputs for boxes, objectness, and class scores[^yolo11-2410-17725-v1-2].
- **Reported:** task family is nano/small/medium/large/xlarge crossed with detection, `-seg`, `-pose`, `-obb`, and `-cls`, each supporting inference, validation, training, and export[^yolo11-2410-17725-v1-3].
- **Reported:** efficiency claims are YOLOv11m higher COCO mAP than YOLOv8m with 22% fewer parameters, YOLOv11x about 54.5% mAP50-95 at 13 ms, YOLOv11s about 47% mAP50-95 in the 2–6 ms low-latency regime, and better scaling across variants with nano for edge through xlarge for high-performance computing[^yolo11-2410-17725-v1-4].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is **small-kernel CSP substitution plus late spatial attention** for accuracy-per-parameter, and a **unified scale-task matrix** for selecting one family across edge-to-cloud deployments; treat latency/AP numbers as vendor-protocol reports, not a shared benchmark.

## YOLOv12 attention-centric design

- **Reported:** identity is `YOLOv12: Attention-Centric Real-Time Object Detectors` by Tian, Ye, and Doermann (Buffalo plus UCAS), arXiv `2502.12524v1` technical report with ICCV 2025 header; family is N/S/M/L/X following YOLOv11 scaling, trained without pretraining or tricks beyond YOLOv11[^yolov12-2502-12524-v1-1].
- **Reported:** diagnosis is quadratic `O(L^2d)` self-attention versus linear `O(kLd)` convolution plus inefficient HBM/SRAM memory access and accumulated overhead from window partition/reversal and positional encodings; design adopts FlashAttention for I/O efficiency[^yolov12-2502-12524-v1-2].
- **Reported:** Area Attention (A2) reshapes `(H,W)` into `l=4` vertical/horizontal areas with no explicit window partition, cutting cost `2n^2hd` to `1/2n^2hd` while keeping `1/4` receptive field; paper finds only slight accuracy impact for large speedup[^yolov12-2502-12524-v1-3].
- **Reported:** R-ELAN adds block-level residual input-to-output with scaling `0.01` plus bottleneck aggregation (one transition, then blocks plus concatenation), versus ELAN split-transition design that blocks gradients; L/X fail to converge without it, while N is unaffected[^yolov12-2502-12524-v1-4].
- **Reported:** adaptations are hierarchical backbone retained (plain only 38.3 AP), first two stages from YOLOv11, single R-ELAN in last stage, `Conv2d+BN` replacing `Linear+LN`, no RPE/APE plus 7x7 separable Position Perceiver on `v`, and MLP ratio `4` to `1.2` (L/X) or `2` (N/S/M) to shift compute toward attention[^yolov12-2502-12524-v1-5].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017` 640x640, T4 TensorRT FP16: N 40.6/56.7/43.8 (6.5G, 2.6M, 1.64 ms) beats YOLOv10-N +2.1 and YOLOv11-N +1.2; S 48.0/65.0/51.8 (21.4G, 9.3M, 2.61 ms) beats RT-DETR-R18 +1.5 and RT-DETRv2-R18 +0.1 at 42% lower latency with 36% FLOPs and 45% params; M 52.5 (67.5G, 20.2M, 4.86 ms); L 53.7 (88.9G, 26.4M, 6.77 ms) beats YOLOv11-L +0.4 and RT-DETR-R50 with ~35% fewer FLOPs and ~37% fewer params; X 55.2/72.0/60.2 (199G, 59.1M, 11.79 ms) beats YOLOv10-X +0.8, YOLOv11-X +0.6, and RT-DETR-R101 with ~23% fewer FLOPs and ~22% fewer params[^yolov12-2502-12524-v1-6].
- **Reported:** ablations without FlashAttention show area attention speedups N/S/X on CUDA FP32/FP16 and CPU (e.g. N 2.7/2.5 to 2.0/2.0 ms, CPU 62.9 to 31.4 ms); with FlashAttention, area attention is 40.6/1.64 versus dense 40.8/1.70, and FlashAttention alone saves N 1.92 to 1.64 and S 3.02 to 2.61 ms; training peaks at 600 epochs (300: 39.5/47.0, 500: 40.3/47.8, 800: 40.4/47.7 N/S)[^yolov12-2502-12524-v1-7].
- **Reported:** limits are FlashAttention-only GPUs (Turing/Ampere/Ada/Hopper such as T4, RTX20/30/40, A5000/6000, A30/40, A100, H100), longer 600-epoch schedule, and ~0.2 AP extra for L/X if separately saved in FP32[^yolov12-2502-12524-v1-7].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is **strip-reshape attention plus residual-scaled aggregation plus convolution-normalized low-MLP attention** inside a hierarchical YOLO backbone — efficiency without giving up large receptive field.

## YOLOv13 hypergraph adaptive correlation

- **Reported:** identity is `YOLOv13: Real-Time Object Detection with Hypergraph-Enhanced Adaptive Visual Perception` by Lei et al., arXiv `2506.17733v2`; diagnosis is YOLO11-and-earlier convolutions aggregate locally while YOLOv12 area attention is still window-limited and pairwise on a fully connected graph, missing global multi-to-multi correlations; prior HyperYOLO-style hypergraphs use a handcrafted feature-distance threshold, causing redundant modeling and weak robustness[^yolov13-2506-17733-v2-1].
- **Reported:** adaptive hypergraph computation replaces binary incidence `H` with continuous participation `A in [0,1]^{N x M}`; global avg plus max pooled context `f_ctx` yields offset `DeltaP=phi(f_ctx)`, prototypes `P=P0+DeltaP`, vertex queries `z_i=W_pre x_i` split into `h` heads with mean similarity `bar s_{i,m}`, then vertex-normalized `A_{i,m}=exp(bar s_{i,m})/sum_j exp(bar s_{j,m})`; convolution is vertex-to-hyperedge `f_m=sigma(W_e sum_i A_{i,m} x_i)` then hyperedge-to-vertex `tilde x_i=sigma(W_v sum_m A_{i,m} f_m)` at linear complexity[^yolov13-2506-17733-v2-2].
- **Reported:** C3AH keeps CSP split: two `1x1` projections to `X` and `X_lateral` with compression `e`, `X_h=AHC(X)`, then concat plus `1x1` fusion; HyperACE resizes `B3/B5` to `B4`, fuses by `1x1` into `X_b`, splits into high-order `X_b^h`, low-order `X_b^l`, shortcut `X_b^s`; high branch uses `K` parallel C3AH concat, low branch uses stacked DS-C3k, then concat plus `1x1` to `Y`[^yolov13-2506-17733-v2-2].
- **Reported:** FullPAD distributes `Y` by resize plus `1x1` to `H_i` and gated fusion `tilde F_i=F_i+gamma H_i` with learnable `gamma`; three tunnels feed seven destinations across backbone-neck, in-neck, and neck-head for fine-grained flow and gradient propagation; lightweight DS family is DSConv plus BN plus SiLU, DS-Bottleneck as `3x3` DS followed by `k x k` DS plus same-channel residual, DS-C3k as CSP with `n` DS-Bottlenecks, DS-C3k2 as C3k2 with DS-C3k, used in backbone/neck and HyperACE low branch for up to 30% fewer params and 28% fewer GFLOPs[^yolov13-2506-17733-v2-3].
- **Reported:** COCO `train2017`/`val2017`, 640x640, 600 epochs, batch 256, SGD lr 0.01 with linear decay plus 3-epoch warmup, Mosaic plus Mixup, `M=4/8/8/12` for N/S/L/X, T4 TensorRT FP16 with reproduced YOLO11/YOLOv12-v1.0 baselines gives N 6.4G/2.5M **41.6/57.8/45.1** 1.97 ms, S 20.8G/9.0M **48.0/65.2/52.0** 2.98 ms, L 88.4G/27.6M **53.4/70.9/58.1** 8.63 ms, X 199.2G/64.0M **54.8/72.0/59.8** 14.67 ms; N/S gains over YOLOv12 are +1.5/+0.9 AP50:95 and +1.8/+1.0 AP50, and over YOLO11-N/S are +3.0/+2.2 AP50:95; S beats RT-DETRv2-R18 47.9 at 20.8 versus 60 GFLOPs and 9.0 versus 20M params[^yolov13-2506-17733-v2-4].
- **Reported:** COCO-trained to VOC2007-test generalization gives N **65.3/83.3/72.4** versus YOLOv12-N 64.3/83.0/71.2 and S **70.1/86.7/77.4** versus YOLOv12-S 69.7/86.5/77.2; qualitative COCO `vis_bbox` claims fewer misses on small bowls/vases, occluded plant behind vase, and racket-versus-shadow cases, while `vis_edge` hyperedges show foreground-foreground groups plus foreground-background links, both via captions and body text without independent visual verification[^yolov13-2506-17733-v2-5].
- **Reported:** S ablations show removing HyperACE/FullPAD drops 48.0/65.2 to 47.1/64.1, while keeping only backbone-neck/in-neck/neck-head gives 47.8/65.0, 47.6/64.8, 47.7/64.9; hyperedge count 2/4/8/16 gives 47.8/47.9/48.0/48.1 AP50:95 so 8 balances cost; DS versus vanilla keeps AP50:95 at 41.6 N and 48.0 S while saving N 1.5G/0.6M and S 6.3G/2.7M; 600 epochs peaks over 300/500/800; T4/4090/CPU-ONNX latencies are N 1.97/1.25/39.97 ms, S 2.98/1.27/61.12 ms, L 8.63/2.36/185.36 ms, X 14.67/3.10/330.12 ms[^yolov13-2506-17733-v2-6].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is **adaptive many-to-many correlation plus full-pipeline redistribution plus depthwise-separable substitution** — learn participation instead of thresholding hyperedges, broadcast the enhanced feature to every pipeline stage with gated residuals, and pay for it with cheaper large-kernel blocks; expect largest wins on nano/small scales and cross-domain robustness rather than X-scale AP jumps.

## YOLO26 edge-first NMS-free YOLO

- **Reported:** identity is `YOLO26: Key Architectural Enhancements and Performance Benchmarking for Real-Time Object Detection` by Sapkota, Cheppally, Sharda, and Karkee (Cornell plus Kansas State), arXiv `2509.25164v5`; it is an analysis/overview of Ultralytics YOLO26 (released September 2025, unveiled at YOLO Vision 2025 in London), framing YOLO26 as deployment-oriented simplification after attention-centric YOLOv12 and hypergraph YOLOv13[^yolo26-2509-25164-v5-1].
- **Reported:** DFL removal turns distributional box decoding into lighter direct regression; paper argues this cuts inference latency and removes export special-casing for ONNX, TensorRT, CoreML, and TFLite, while YOLOv12/YOLOv13 retain DFL and pay latency plus export costs on constrained devices[^yolo26-2509-25164-v5-2].
- **Reported:** native end-to-end NMS-free head outputs direct non-redundant boxes with no IoU-threshold post-processing; paper claims up to 43% faster CPU inference for the nano model versus prior YOLO, plus simpler threshold-free deployment and better reproducibility, and presents YOLO26 as the first YOLO release to adopt NMS-free inference while keeping YOLO speed–accuracy balance (YOLOv13 still depends on NMS)[^yolo26-2509-25164-v5-2].
- **Reported:** Ultralytics documentation clarifies that this path is optional rather than the runtime default: prediction and validation use the higher-accuracy one-to-many head plus NMS unless `nms=False` selects the one-to-one head. The documented raw output changes from `(N, nc + 4, 8400)` to at most 300 detections shaped `(N, 300, 6)`; both heads are trained regardless of the inference choice[^ultralytics-yolo26-doc-9f4ac453-1].
- **Reported:** The documentation cites `Ultralytics YOLO26: Unified Real-Time End-to-End Vision Models` (`arXiv:2606.03748`) as the complete technical paper. It was not available in the local source bundle; the already compiled `arXiv:2509.25164v5` is an independent analysis/overview, not that official paper[^ultralytics-yolo26-doc-9f4ac453-1].
- **Reported:** ProgLoss progressively rebalances loss-component weights against easy-example domination late in training, while STAL explicitly prioritizes label assignment for tiny or occluded instances; paper contrasts this lightweight training-side fix with YOLOv12/v13 attention and fusion blocks that raise inference cost for similar small-object gains[^yolo26-2509-25164-v5-3].
- **Reported:** MuSGD hybridizes SGD generalization with Muon-inspired momentum/curvature behavior from LLM training practice; paper reports faster, smoother convergence in fewer epochs with less hyperparameter sensitivity than the SGD/AdamW setups of YOLOv8–v13, hence shorter development cycles and fewer restarts[^yolo26-2509-25164-v5-3].
- **Reported:** COCO detection at 640 px, CPU ONNX versus T4 TensorRT10 FP16 with params/FLOPs — N 40.9/40.1 e2e, 38.9 ms / 1.7 ms, 2.4M/5.4B; S 48.6/47.8, 87.2 ms / 2.5 ms, 9.5M/20.7B; M 53.1/52.5, 220.0 ms / 4.7 ms, 20.4M/68.2B; L 55.0/54.4, 286.2 ms / 6.2 ms, 24.8M/86.4B; X 57.5/56.9 e2e, 525.8 ms / 11.8 ms, 55.7M/193.9B[^yolo26-2509-25164-v5-4].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is **simplify inference, densify training**: DFL-free regression plus one-to-one NMS-free head for deployment, ProgLoss plus STAL plus MuSGD for stability and small-target recall — the YOLO counterpart to the DETR train-dense/infer-sparse pattern above.

## RT-DETRv4 training-only VFM distillation

- **Reported:** identity is `RT-DETRv4: Painlessly Furthering Real-Time Object Detection with Vision Foundation Models` by Liao et al. (PKU Shenzhen plus Tsinghua), arXiv `2510.25257v1`; it adds a Deep Semantic Injector (DSI) plus Gradient-guided Adaptive Modulation (GAM) to RT-DETR-family detectors, training-only with no inference-architecture change and no deployment overhead[^rtdetrv4-2510-25257-v1-1].
- **Reported:** diagnosis is the `F5 Semantic Bottleneck`: in the RT-DETR hybrid encoder only `S5` passes through self-attention (AIFI) to make `F5`, which then conditions CCFF fusion, query selection, and decoding, yet `F5` receives only indirect detection-loss gradients through decoder plus CCFF[^rtdetrv4-2510-25257-v1-2].
- **Reported:** DSI aligns only AIFI output `F5` with a frozen VFM teacher: ViT patch tokens are reshaped to 2D, interpolated to `F5` resolution, and matched by a lightweight projector on `F5`, supervised by mean patch-wise negative cosine similarity; total loss is `L_total = L_det + λ L_DSI` with gradients left attached so `F5` supervision flows back through AIFI and backbone[^rtdetrv4-2510-25257-v1-2].
- **Reported:** injection-position ablations favor this targeted choice: aligning backbone `S3`/`S4`/`S5` alone or jointly gives no gain and hybrid backbone-plus-`F5` alignment stalls at baseline 53.8 AP, while `F5`-only reaches 54.3 AP; paper reads joint CNN-backbone plus Transformer-AIFI alignment as an optimization or semantic conflict[^rtdetrv4-2510-25257-v1-5].
- **Reported:** GAM replaces fixed `λ` with per-epoch control of the AIFI gradient share: per-step L1 gradient norms for backbone/AIFI/CCFF/decoder give ratio `r_t = G_t(AIFI)/G_t(total)`, averaged to `\bar{r}_e`, then `λ` is scaled toward the nearer edge of `[ρ−δ, ρ+δ]` and frozen inside the interval; `ρ` sets supervision intensity and `δ` trades responsiveness against stability[^rtdetrv4-2510-25257-v1-3].
- **Reported:** COCO `train2017`/`val2017` setup uses a frozen DINOv3-ViT-B teacher, baseline-consistent hyperparameters from RT-DETRv2/D-FINE/DEIM, and T4 TensorRT FP16 latency; SOTA Tab. reports **S 49.7, M 53.5, L 55.4, X 57.0 AP** at **273/169/124/78 FPS** with unchanged params/FLOPs/latency versus the corresponding DEIM scales[^rtdetrv4-2510-25257-v1-4].
- **Reported:** same table has X beating DEIM-X 56.5 and YOLOv12-X 55.2, L 55.4 beating DEIM-L 54.7 and YOLOv13-L 53.4, and M 53.5 beating DEIM-M 52.7; DEIMv2 is shown only in the AP-latency figure because its latency regime differs, with M still ahead of DEIMv2-S 53.5 versus 50.9 at 169 versus 173 FPS[^rtdetrv4-2510-25257-v1-4].
- **Reported:** DSI-plus-GAM transfers across detectors at 36 epochs: RT-DETRv2-L 52.1→52.6 (+0.5), D-FINE-L 53.1→53.4 (+0.3), DEIM-L 53.8→54.3 (+0.5), with DSI alone only +0.1–0.2; projector ablation favors linear over MLP/1×1 conv, cosine loss beats MSE (DEIM-M 90-epoch +12-epoch EMA: 53.5 versus 52.7 versus 52.5 baseline), and GAM 55.4 beats the best static `λ=20` 55.1 with faster early-to-mid convergence[^rtdetrv4-2510-25257-v1-5].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is **supervise the semantic bottleneck, modulate by gradient share**: pick the single deepest global feature for VFM alignment, let its gradient update the encoder path, and control the auxiliary weight by component-gradient ratio rather than loss magnitude — a training-only complement to dense matching and distributional localization above.

## RF-DETR weight-sharing NAS for per-dataset Pareto

- **Reported:** identity is `RF-DETR: Neural Architecture Search for Real-Time Detection Transformers` by Robinson, Robicheaux, Popov (Roboflow) plus Ramanan and Peri (CMU), ICLR 2026 style, arXiv `2511.09554v2`; thesis is specialist detectors are over-optimized for COCO through bespoke LR/augmentation schedulers and architectures, while fine-tuned VLMs pay heavy text-encoder runtime and lose open-vocabulary generality[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-1].
- **Reported:** architecture modernizes LW-DETR: CAEv2 backbone replaced by DINOv2, batch norm replaced by layer norm in the multi-scale projector to allow gradient accumulation on consumer GPUs, window blocks at layers `{0,1,3,4,6,7,9,10}` versus LW-DETR `{0,1,3,6,7,9}`, LR `1e-4` with batch 128, gradient clip `0.1`, per-layer decay `0.8`, EMA scheduler without warm-up, symmetric multi-scale `0.5–1.5`, and batch-level resize to cut padding and window artifacts[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-2].
- **Reported:** end-to-end weight-sharing NAS samples one random configuration per training iteration, then grid-searches the validation set at inference without retraining; knobs are patch size (FlexiViT-style interpolation; train `8,10,12,16,20,24,32`, inference adds `14`), resolution `320–960` step `64`, decoder layers `0–6` dropped at inference, queries `50,100,200,300` dropped lowest-first by encoder max-sigmoid confidence, and windows per block `1,2,4`; total search is `11×7×7×3×4=6,468` configs at about `10,000` GPU-hours (`200×T4×48h`), with `2–4×` baseline training cost for a single run that yields all sizes[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-3].
- **Reported:** COCO `val2017` detection (T4 TensorRT, same-artifact protocol): N `48.0` AP (`30.5M`, `31.9` GFLOPs, `2.3` ms) beats D-FINE-N `42.7` by `5.3` and LW-DETR-T `42.9` by `5.1`; S `52.9` (`32.1M`, `59.8`, `3.5` ms) beats D-FINE-S `50.6`; M `54.7` (`33.7M`, `78.8`, `4.4` ms) near D-FINE-M `55.0`; L `56.5`/`6.8` ms, XL `58.6`/`11.5` ms, 2XL `60.1` (`126.9M`, `438.4`, `17.2` ms) claimed first real-time detector above `60` AP, Max `61.8`/`98.0` ms[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-4].
- **Reported:** Pareto configs on COCO fix patch/windows/queries within a family and scale resolution plus decoder depth: N `384/16/2-win/2-dec/300q/DINOv2-S`, S `512/16/2/3/300/S`, M `576/16/2/4/300/S`, L `704/16/2/4/300/S`, XL `700/20/1/5/300/B`, 2XL `880/20/2/5/300/B`, Max `828/12/1/6/300/B`[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-4].
- **Reported:** RF100-VL averaged over 100 datasets: N `57.8` (`58.6` with fine-tuning)/`2.5` ms, S `60.9` (`61.2`)/`3.7` ms, M `61.7` (`62.0`)/`4.6` ms, L `62.0` (`62.3`)/`6.2` ms, XL `62.6` (`63.0`)/`9.7` ms, 2XL `63.3` (`63.5`)/`15.6` ms, beating fine-tuned GroundingDINO-T `62.3`/`309.9` ms\* and LLMDet-T `62.3`/`308.4` ms\* at roughly `20×` lower latency; RT-DETR beats D-FINE at AP50, read as D-FINE hyperparameter overfit to COCO, while YOLOv8/11 do not improve with scale on RF100-VL[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-5].
- **Reported:** ablations on M: LW-DETR-M `52.6` goes to `51.6` with gentler hyperparameters (lower LR plus layer norm costs accuracy but preserves DINOv2 and enables larger batches), `53.6` with DINOv2 backbone, `54.3` with extra Objects-365 pretraining, `54.6` with weight-sharing NAS; NAS therefore helps even the base config, interpreted as architecture-augmentation regularization[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-6].
- **Reported:** inference-time flexibility: dropping the last decoder costs about `10%` latency for `2` AP, dropping all decoders yields a single-stage YOLO-style NMS-free detector without cross/self-attention; dropping the `100` lowest-confidence queries barely changes AP while saving latency; post-NAS fine-tuning is `+0.4` N, `+0.1` S, `+0.0` M on COCO but larger on RF100-VL, especially for small models needing more than `100` epochs[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-6].
- **Reported:** latency-benchmarking protocol: `200` ms buffering between forwards stabilizes power-throttling variance, accuracy and latency must use the same artifact, YOLO TensorRT mAP is irreproducible because eval uses multi-class NMS while inference uses single-class NMS plus `FP32→FP16` degradation, naive D-FINE FP16 collapses to `0.5` AP and is fixed with ONNX opset `17`, CUDA graphs speed RT-DETR/LW-DETR/RF-DETR but not D-FINE, and TensorRT recompilation still leaves about `0.1` ms variance[^rfdetr-2511-09554-v2-7].
- **Synthesis:** durable reuse is **train once, search many**: keep a strong DINOv2 specialist with scheduler-free training, expose resolution/patch/windows/decoder/queries as jointly searchable inference knobs, and select per-dataset operating points by validation grid search with optional small-model fine-tuning.
