Heilbroner frames *The Worldly Philosophers* as a history of ideas that shaped society: economists seek order and meaning in the historically situated production and distribution of wealth, and their theories can influence political regimes, conflicts, and daily life[^heilbroner-001]. The revised seventh edition adds a unifying lens: changing preanalytic social *visions* behind social analysis, rather than mere chronology, connect the thinkers' distinct accounts[^heilbroner-001].

# Scope of the worldly philosophy

- The book is not a catalogue of principles. It follows history-shaping ideas alongside the people and social settings from which they arose; the thinkers are united less by personality or doctrine than by curiosity about how people create and contest material wealth[^heilbroner-001].
- In Heilbroner's account, this makes them “worldly philosophers”: they try to bring the drive for wealth, and apparently disconnected figures such as workers, speculators, shopkeepers, and crowds, within an explanatory social philosophy[^heilbroner-001].
- The inquiry is historical rather than timeless. Although humans long faced material problems, economists emerged only in the modern age; explaining that earlier world and its transition is therefore a necessary prelude to Adam Smith and later thinkers[^heilbroner-001].

# Social vision as an organizing lens

For the seventh edition, Heilbroner distinguishes routine corrections and altered emphases from the consequential revision: he now treats evolving “visions” behind social analysis as the thread that ties the book together[^heilbroner-001]. He attributes the initial idea to Joseph Schumpeter's 1950s work, while noting that Schumpeter did not himself apply it to the history of economic thought[^heilbroner-001]. Heilbroner deliberately postpones its implications for the present until the concluding chapter, so the source presents this as the book's interpretive architecture rather than a settled general theory[^heilbroner-001].

# Authorial and edition context

- Heilbroner describes the 1999 revised seventh edition as the seventh revision of a book begun roughly forty-six years earlier while he was a graduate student at the New School for Social Research[^heilbroner-001].
- He credits Adolph Lowe's seminar on Adam Smith with identifying the subject and Frederick Lewis Allen with proposing the title *The Worldly Philosophers*[^heilbroner-001]. These are the author's retrospective accounts, not independently verified biographical claims.
- He expected this to be the last edition; the preface is dated July 1998[^heilbroner-001].
