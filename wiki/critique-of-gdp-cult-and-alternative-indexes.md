# Critique of the GDP cult and alternative indexes

GDP statistics are useful diagnostic tools, not a national goal; attacks that conflate measuring market production with surrendering community values commit a bait-and-switch, and alternative single-number rankings replicate GDP's aggregation problems while adding ideological opacity[^harford-cult-gnp].

## Two flavors of anti-growth argument

Harford separates concerns often conflated[^harford-cult-gnp]:
1. **GDP is a flawed welfare measure** — we should measure something more useful instead (e.g., happiness).
2. **Growth has physical limits** — we must learn to live without growth (addressed in the following chapter, not here; see [Exponential growth, Malthusian limits, and the physicists' critique](exponential-growth-malthusian-limits-and-physicists-critique.md), [Decoupling economic growth from energy and material throughput](decoupling-economic-growth-from-energy-and-material-throughput.md), and [Zero-growth economy — leisure, debt, and pricing real constraints](zero-growth-leisure-debt-and-carbon-pricing.md)).

This concept addresses (1).

## Kennedy's critique and the bait-and-switch

Robert F. Kennedy (1968, GNP >$800bn): GNP counts air pollution, cigarette advertising, ambulances, locks and jails, redwood destruction, napalm and nuclear warheads, etc., but not children's health, education, play, poetry, marriage strength, public debate, or integrity — "it measures everything... except that which makes life worthwhile"[^harford-cult-gnp].

Harford: Kennedy is right that important things are omitted, but wrong to imply economists care only about those figures[^harford-cult-gnp]. The rhetorical move — "we surrendered excellence... why? Because statistical offices since the early 1930s gather production estimates" — mistakes measurement for cause[^harford-cult-gnp]. Chapter 1's short answer: growth gives means to tackle inequality, environment, happiness; the chapter gives the fuller answer[^harford-cult-gnp][^harford-ch4].

## Why GDP is not a policy fetish

- **Growth predates measurement.** The late-19th-century transformation — industrial farming, coal, urban factory migration — produced unprecedented growth before GDP existed; economists could see it but not measure it[^harford-cult-gnp].
- **Governments do not optimize GDP.** Ministers do not wake up asking how to raise GDP. Examples from the month of writing: UK debates on leaving the EU, pension simplification/redistribution, leaving a flaw in inflation calculation uncorrected, education reforms — all with redistributive, institutional or outcome motives, none referencing GDP[^harford-cult-gnp]. Politicians track fairness, prices, public services, freedom, unemployment — at best correlated with GDP[^harford-cult-gnp].
- **Rankings have little policy traction.** A GDP/GNI per capita league would top Qatar, Luxembourg, Liechtenstein, Monaco or Bermuda — none offering transferable lessons[^harford-cult-gnp]. The same applies to ideological rankings (see below).

## What statistics are for

Statistics help make better decisions when they illuminate an actionable problem, not when they produce PR rankings[^harford-cult-gnp]. Disaggregated measures — inflation, inequality, unemployment, GDP — are each useful without being collapsed into one amorphous summary; all aggregates compromise (e.g., inflation's "typical" basket, part-time work in employment) but that does not require a single welfare number[^harford-cult-gnp].

Better approach for non-GDP concerns: measure the specific problem (domestic violence, species extinction, child literacy) and commission quality research such as randomized policy trials; imputing a "psychic cost" of domestic violence into GDP is possible but rarely the best way to address it, and omitting it from GDP does not signal indifference[^harford-cult-gnp].

## Alternative indexes as advocacy

Many organizations publish rankings for media attention; each ranking encodes the publisher's agenda[^harford-cult-gnp]:

- **Happy Planet Index (New Economics Foundation, 2006)** — claimed Vanuatu was happiest; press repeated without reading methodology[^harford-cult-gnp].
  - Formula: (life satisfaction 1–10 × life expectancy) / ecological footprint[^harford-cult-gnp]. Vanuatu: 68.6 years × 7.4 / 1.1 = 461 "bliss-years per footprint"[^harford-cult-gnp]. US footprint 9.5 would require 4,389 bliss-years to score 462; at maximum satisfaction 10, US life expectancy would need to be 439 years[^harford-cult-gnp].
  - Critique: measures environmental efficiency per the NEF agenda, not happiness; footnote data did not exist — Vanuatu's 7.4 was imputed from "comparable" countries due to tiny population (<1/10 of Brooklyn)[^harford-cult-gnp]. US could top the index only by drastic footprint reduction or impossible longevity[^harford-cult-gnp].
- **Index of Economic Freedom (Heritage Foundation)** — Hong Kong on top; reflects pro-market agenda[^harford-cult-gnp].
- **Human Development Index (UNDP)** — Norway on top; weighted income, education, life expectancy[^harford-cult-gnp][^harford-ch4].
- **Corruption Perceptions Index (Transparency International)** — Denmark top, Somalia/Afghanistan bottom; reflects anti-corruption agenda[^harford-cult-gnp].

Lesson: because statistical offices compile GDP, it does not follow governments care only about GDP — any more than Heritage cares only about freedom or UNDP only about human development[^harford-cult-gnp]. Happiness can be measured directly for policy consideration, but that is distinct from replacing GDP (next chapter)[^harford-cult-gnp].
