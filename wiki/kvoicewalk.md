KVoiceWalk is an open-source Python tool that searches for new [Kokoro-82M](kokoro-82m.md) voice style tensors (`.pt` files) whose synthesized speech sounds closer to a supplied target recording. It starts from a population of existing voices, or from an interpolated population, mutates candidates by random walk, scores each candidate with a hybrid similarity measure, and keeps the best. The author presents it as proof of the scoring idea and a step toward a genetic algorithm, not as a finished cloning product. The README reports one example run in which the closest stock voice reached target similarity 0.709 and the walked tensor reached 0.917; neither figure is independently checked here (**Reported**; **Unverified**).[^kvoicewalk-readme]

## Approach

- **Goal:** evolve Kokoro voice tensors toward target audio, because the Kokoro style tensors are small and the author wanted more voice options (lines 2–4).[^kvoicewalk-readme]
- **Starting population:** the voices folder (`./voices`), limited to 10 voices by default. The optional interpolation search (`--interpolate_start`) looks for better starting voices among blends of trained voices, saves them to an `interpolated` folder, and then continues the walk (lines 77–84).[^kvoicewalk-readme]
- **Walk:** standard-deviation mutation of a supplied voice tensor, with the interpolated population as its basis; the walk replaces the current voice with the best candidate (line 95).[^kvoicewalk-readme]
- **Scoring:** a hybrid of target similarity, self-similarity, and audio-feature similarity, described in [KVoiceWalk Scoring Function](kvoicewalk-scoring-function.md) (lines 101–106).[^kvoicewalk-readme]
- **Companion modes:** Faster-Whisper transcription of the target, voice-tensor export to `voices.bin`, and a test mode that renders `out.wav` from a `.pt` voice (lines 72–76, 118–141).[^kvoicewalk-readme] Commands are in [KVoiceWalk Usage and Arguments](kvoicewalk-usage-and-arguments.md).

## Performance and hardware

- The project was tested on an NVIDIA RTX 3060 (line 15). The author reports about 4 GB of VRAM in CUDA mode, about 26 hours on CPU, and about 4 hours on an RTX 3060, a 6.5x speedup on the author's setup (lines 205–208).[^kvoicewalk-readme] The timings do not state target length or population size; the only stated condition is the 10,000-step example command (line 200).
- One instance does not run in parallel; the author runs about two instances at once on a 3070 laptop GPU (line 109).[^kvoicewalk-readme]
- Device selection covers Kokoro synthesis, Resemblyzer scoring, and Faster-Whisper transcription (lines 179–187).[^kvoicewalk-readme]

## Reliability

The author says outcomes are random: some runs produced good voices after long stagnation, while others "crash and burn right away" (line 109). The README offers no multi-run statistics, so the example table is a single success rather than a typical outcome (**Reported**; **Synthesis**).

The author also says that feature similarity kept audio from degrading when the first two checks passed, and that the harmonic mean avoided stagnation (lines 104–106). These design claims are qualitative in the README and unmeasured.[^kvoicewalk-readme]
