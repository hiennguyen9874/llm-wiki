The market system was not planned or welcomed. Heilbroner describes its birth as an agony from the thirteenth century well into the nineteenth, in which commercialized land, labor and capital were ground out over the wails of guilds, lords and peasants, against frantic resistance[^heilbroner-002].

# Synthesis

Frightened defenders of the old embedded order fought commodification at every step, while deep political, religious, material and scientific currents nevertheless ripped custom apart. Early philosophies — especially mercantilism — tried to impose intellectual order on the emerging scramble by centering on gold and national power, but remained contradictory until Adam Smith in 1776 supplied the first coherent tableau of a self-regulating commercial society[^heilbroner-002][^heilbroner-003].

# Resistance to commodification

*   **Capital's dread of change (France 1666):** A weaver wishing to try an innovation must obtain permission from town judges after consideration by four oldest merchants and four oldest weavers — ensuring few proposals[^heilbroner-002]. Button-makers demand right to search homes/wardrobes and arrest street wearers of novel cloth buttons; government fines cloth-button makers. Earlier, Privy Council denies and abolishes stocking-frame patent; later same Council will forbid its export seventy years later. Importation of printed calicoes costs 16,000 lives; in Valence alone 77 hanged, 58 broken on the wheel, 631 sent to galleys, one freed for dealing in forbidden calicoes[^heilbroner-002].
*   **Labor's dispossession — enclosure:** End sixteenth century, Queen Elizabeth laments "Paupers are everywhere!" A century earlier the countryside had been yeomen proprietors. Wool profitability drives enclosure of common lands and the crazy-quilt scattered holdings (marked only by tree or rock) into private property of the lord. John Hales 1549: "where XL persons had their lyvings, now one man and his shepherd hath all.... those shepe is the cause of all theise meschieves"[^heilbroner-002]. Riots as early as mid-sixteenth century kill 3,500; process still full swing mid-eighteenth, not complete until mid-nineteenth. 1820 Duchess of Sutherland evicts 15,000 tenants from 794,000 acres, replaces with 131,000 sheep, allots evicted families average 2 acres submarginal each[^heilbroner-002].
*   Peasant becomes agricultural proletarian, beggar, robber, pauper. Parliament localizes relief (ties paupers to parishes for pittance, whips/brands/mutilates wanderers); contemporary clergyman around Smith's time calls workhouses "Houses of Terror." Tying to parish prevents the only solution — a fluid mobile labor force following market dictates. Perhaps not heartlessness but incomprehension of a labor market[^heilbroner-002].

Result: economic land created from ancestral estates, economic labor from unemployed apprentices and dispossessed farm laborers, economic capital from timid guild masters taught jungle laws. Transactions became the new motive force — the great chariot formerly running down tradition's gentle slope now powered by an internal combustion machine[^heilbroner-002].

# Four currents enabling the transformation

No single massive cause; the new way grew inside the old like butterfly in chrysalis, sprawling over time with overlaps (guilds/feudal privileges abolished France 1790, England's Statute of Artificers repealed 1813) but substantially ascendant by 1700[^heilbroner-002].

1.  **Emergence of national political units.** Peasant wars and kingly conquest replace isolated feudalism with centralized monarchies and national spirit. Royal patronage (French tapestry works), armadas/armies and satellite industries, national laws/common measurements/standardized currencies replace the infinity of local tolls and measures that plagued travelers like Andreas Ryff. Foreign adventure shifts from private (Polo brothers thirteenth century to great Khan unprotected) to national (Columbus under Isabella fifteenth century). Treasure flood and treasure-consciousness follow: Columbus — "He who has gold, makes and accomplishes whatever he wishes in the world and finally uses it to send souls into paradise;" Queen Elizabeth's share as stockholder in Drake's *Golden Hynd* voyage paid off all England's foreign debts, balanced its budget, and invested abroad a sum which at compound interest accounted for Britain's entire 1930 overseas wealth[^heilbroner-002].
2.  **Decay of religious spirit / Renaissance humanism and Protestantism.** Rise of Today over Tomorrow, material comforts; Roman Church's dubious view of merchants and sin of usury re-evaluated as merchants became integral. Protestant leaders preach making most of God-given talents in daily business for God's glory — acquisitiveness becomes virtue, then riches identified with spiritual excellence. Contrast twelfth-century folk tale of usurer crushed by falling statue of another usurer entering church to marry (God's displeasure), and 1639 Keayne collision with Puritans, with later acceptance essential for market expansion[^heilbroner-002].
3.  **Material changes enabling market life.** Feudal age's slow monetization — 1,000 towns in 500 years, rudimentary roads, food supply linking — builds familiarity with money; power gravitates to merchants. Need for rational accounting: Venetians using sophisticated devices twelfth century, but most merchants still schoolboys; double entry not standard until seventeenth century, prerequisite for large-scale business[^heilbroner-002].
4.  **Rise of scientific curiosity.** Sub-industrial discoveries — printing press, paper mill, windmill, mechanical clock, map — and new friendliness to experimentation and innovation, preparing ground for later Industrial Revolution[^heilbroner-002].

By 1700 the get-rich-quick fever is visible: John Law's 1718 Mississippi Company in Paris, Scottish adventurer selling shares in American gold mountains — street fights for shares, murders, overnight fortunes (one waiter 30 million *livres*), government parades 1,000 beggars with picks/shovels as fake miners to stave collapse on Rue de Quincampoix. The system would be called capitalism, though word not widely used until late nineteenth century, and gain soon affirmed as eternal human nature[^heilbroner-002].

# Visions before Smith — mercantilism to commerce

Every age breeds philosophers to tell itself its society mirrors providence. Before Smith, attention was political, not economic. When the perplexing marketplace finally demanded explanation, visions for two hundred years tried to order it[^heilbroner-002][^heilbroner-003]:

*   **Mercantilist core:** national power as natural object, gold as most important ingredient. Pamphleteers equate treasure-hunting adventurers (Columbus, Cortes, Drake) with economic progress. Thomas Hobbes *Leviathan* (1651) frontispiece — giant king in armor of human heads towering over countryside — embodies political vision: all-powerful state prevents life "solitary, poore, nasty, brutish, and short." Mercantilists support strong state but fear merchant ships bringing foreign luxuries draining bullion[^heilbroner-002].
*   Even before *Leviathan*, commercial spokesmen argue trade enriches sovereign: Thomas Mun, director of East India Company, *England's Treasure by Forraign Trade* — ordinary means to increase wealth is to "sell more to strangers yearly than wee consume of theirs in Value"[^heilbroner-002][^heilbroner-003].
*   By eighteenth century emphasis shifts from gold to *commerce* as vitality; new question becomes how to create wealth by assisting merchants — and how to keep poor poor, for without poverty they would demand exorbitant wages. Bernard Mandeville: "To make the Society Happy..., it is requisite that great numbers should be Ignorant as well as Poor"[^heilbroner-003].
*   Melee of contradictory pamphleteers, parsons, cranks — some saying never buy more than you sell, others opposite; some exalting trader, others farmer; some seeing poverty as divinely ordained wealth-creator, others as evil. Dr. Samuel Johnson: "There is nothing which requires more to be illustrated by philosophy than trade does"[^heilbroner-003].
*   **Smith's synthesis:** *Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* published 1776 — same year as American political democracy — unfolds economic blueprint; Smith, conservative in self-view, nevertheless supplies the image of a self-regulating commercial society proceeding at majestic pace toward a visible goal, becoming spectacles for generations[^heilbroner-003].
