# Federal funds rate, discount rate, and the Fed's money-supply target — how open-market operations hit the FOMC target

In recent years the Fed has conducted monetary policy by choosing a target for the **federal funds rate** — the overnight interbank rate — and using open-market operations to hit it; announcing a funds-rate change *is* committing to the money-supply change needed to achieve it — two sides of the same coin[^mankiw-16-funds][^krugman-ch15-083].

## Central-bank-money equilibrium model

In Blanchard's simplified deposits-only model, people demand checkable deposits according to $M^d=YL(i)$. If banks hold a reserve ratio $\theta$, their demand for central-bank money (reserves) is $H^d=\theta M^d=\theta YL(i)$. With central-bank supply $H$, equilibrium is $H=\theta YL(i)$: an increase in $H$ lowers the interest rate needed to make banks and the public willingly hold the additional central-bank money.[^blanchard-semantic-traps]

This is a model of the reserve-market mechanism, not a claim that contemporary implementation always operates through a fixed required reserve ratio. Figure 4-7, inspected in the source, depicts the vertical supply of central-bank money and downward-sloping demand.[^blanchard-semantic-traps]

## What is the federal funds rate?

- The federal funds rate is the short-term interest rate banks charge one another for loans, typically **overnight**, to cover reserve shortfalls[^mankiw-16-funds].
- Definition from Mankiw glossary: *federal funds rate — the interest rate at which banks make overnight loans to one another*[^mankiw-16-funds].
- Mechanics: one bank short of reserves borrows reserves from a bank with excess reserves; the price of that temporary loan is the federal funds rate[^mankiw-16-funds]. Krugman/Wells likewise describes the federal funds market as usually overnight borrowing by banks below their reserve requirement from banks with excess reserves[^krugman-ch14-078].

## How it differs from the discount rate

- The **discount rate** is the interest rate banks pay to borrow **directly from the Federal Reserve** through the **discount window**[^mankiw-16-funds].
- Borrowing in the federal funds market vs borrowing from the Fed are alternatives; a bank short of reserves will do whichever is cheaper[^mankiw-16-funds].
- In practice the two rates move closely together[^mankiw-16-funds]. Krugman/Wells presents the Fed's normal discount rate as one percentage point above the federal funds rate to discourage routine use of the window, but reports narrower spreads in 2008 (0.25 percentage points) and early 2020 (0.60 points); these are source-era observations[^krugman-ch14-078].

## Why it matters beyond banks

- Only banks borrow directly in the federal funds market, but its economic impact is much broader because different parts of the financial system are highly interconnected and loan rates are strongly correlated[^mankiw-16-funds].
- When the federal funds rate rises or falls, other interest rates often move in the same direction[^mankiw-16-funds].

## What the Fed has to do with it — FOMC target

- In recent years the Fed has **set a target goal** for the federal funds rate[^mankiw-16-funds].
- When the **Federal Open Market Committee meets approximately every 6 weeks**, it decides whether to raise or lower that target[^mankiw-16-funds].

## How the Fed hits the target — open-market operations and reserves

- Although the actual federal funds rate is set by supply and demand in the market for interbank reserve loans, the Fed can **influence that market with open-market operations**[^mankiw-16-funds]:
  - **Fed buys bonds → injects reserves** → fewer banks need to borrow reserves to meet reserve requirements → demand for borrowing reserves falls → price (federal funds rate) **decreases**[^mankiw-16-funds].
  - **Fed sells bonds → withdraws reserves** → more banks find themselves short of reserves → they bid up the price of borrowing reserves → federal funds rate **increases**[^mankiw-16-funds].
- Summary: open-market **purchases lower** the federal funds rate; open-market **sales raise** it[^mankiw-16-funds].

## Post-QE reserve-rate operating framework (source-era)

After the Fed's crisis asset purchases left a much larger stock of reserves, Blanchard describes the late-2015 operating problem: without interest on reserves, banks would prefer to lend excess reserves and the funds rate would tend toward zero unless the Fed sold its acquired assets. The source says the Fed instead used the rate on reserves as the key policy tool while retaining the balance sheet.[^blanchard-097]

In that account, the reserve rate forms the effective floor and the discount rate a ceiling for the funds rate. Its 2015–early-2019 chart shows the funds rate tracking the reserve rate but slightly below it, a technical result of some institutions being unable to hold Fed reserves and accepting lower rates.[^blanchard-097] This describes the source-period framework, not current implementation.

## Two sides of the same coin — funds rate and money supply

- Do open-market operations affect the money supply? *Yes, absolutely*[^mankiw-16-funds].
- When the Fed announces a change in the federal funds rate it is committing to the open-market operations necessary to make that change happen, and those operations alter the supply of money[^mankiw-16-funds].
- Decisions by the FOMC to change the target for the federal funds rate are **also decisions to change the money supply** — they are two sides of the same coin[^mankiw-16-funds].
- Other things equal: a **decrease** in the target → **expansion** in money supply; an **increase** in the target → **contraction** in money supply[^mankiw-16-funds].

## Using all tools to contract — QuickQuiz synthesis

- Mankiw QuickQuiz: If the Fed wanted to use *all* its policy tools to decrease the money supply, what would it do? Synthesis from 16-4a–d[^mankiw-16-funds]:
  - **Open-market sale** of government bonds (withdraw reserves)[^mankiw-16-funds].
  - **Increase** the discount rate and decrease direct lending / TAF lending (raise cost / reduce quantity of borrowed reserves)[^mankiw-16-funds].
  - **Raise** reserve requirements (raise reserve ratio, lower multiplier)[^mankiw-16-funds].
  - **Increase** the interest rate paid on reserves (encourage holding reserves, raise ratio, lower multiplier)[^mankiw-16-funds].
  - Raise the **target** for the federal funds rate — the observable counterpart of the contraction[^mankiw-16-funds].
