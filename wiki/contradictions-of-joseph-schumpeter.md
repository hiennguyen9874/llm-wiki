Heilbroner's "The Contradictions of Joseph Schumpeter" juxtaposes Keynes's 1930 *Economic Possibilities for Our Grandchildren* — a 2030 plenty at 7.5× wealth — with Schumpeter's entrepreneurial theory of capitalist development to ask where capitalism stands, then follows Schumpeter's life from Theresianum schoolboy to Harvard's cloaked lecturer, his statics-then-dynamics of the circular flow and profit, his swarm-driven business cycles tested by the Great Depression, and his 1942 *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy* prognosis that capitalism will economically succeed but sociologically fail and be succeeded by a managerial socialism still staffed by its bourgeois elite, framed finally as a theory of elites and of preanalytic vision[^heilbroner-018].

# Synthesis

1930 Keynes glimpsed, amid depression, a 2030 solving of scarcity if productivity continued at its past-century pace; Heilbroner uses that optimism as foil for Schumpeter (1883–1950), who agreed capitalism was intrinsically dynamic and needed no permanent fiscal prop yet concluded "Can capitalism survive? No" — not for Marx's economic reasons but because rationality, bureaucratization, and loss of bourgeois faith would hollow it from within. Schumpeter's analytic route — a profitless circular flow disrupted by entrepreneurs bearing innovations whose transient rents explain profit, interest, credit and swarming booms — underwrites his "perennial gale of creative destruction" and three interlinked cycles, but Heilbroner judges the long-run vision sociological speculation rather than economic law: prescient about technology and managerial capitalism, blind to technology's dangers and multinationals' continued aggressiveness, and inverted by the late-century revival of market faith[^heilbroner-018].

# Keynes's 2030 plenty as foil

- *Economic Possibilities for Our Grandchildren* (1930) — even as Keynes warned "in the long run we are all dead," he projected that if the last hundred years' pace continued, Britain's real product would be 7.5× in another century, scarcity as an economic problem could be solved, and the new problem would be coping with unprecedented leisure — quoted old charwoman's epitaph: "Don't mourn for me... For I'm going to do nothing for ever and ever" with psalms ringing but nothing to do with singing[^heilbroner-018].
- Growth logic: not Nature's generosity — diminishing returns — but each generation's inherited equipment, factories, tools and knowledge steadily raising productivity; 1960s U.S. factory worker a Superman to post–Civil War grandfather; another century of same amassing would replicate that leap[^heilbroner-018].
- Not taken seriously in 1930 clanking machinery, and Keynes himself turned to immediate unemployment; yet Heilbroner marks it as first turn from history to our own futures, posing three great questions — where capitalism stands, signposts ahead, our children's inheritance — for the modern economists beginning with Schumpeter[^heilbroner-018].
- Harvard anecdote — Schumpeter strode into depression lecture in European cloak: "Chentlemen, you are vorried about the depression. You should not be. For capitalism, a depression is a good cold *douche*" (Schumpeter's former student Heilbroner notes classmates did not know *douche* = shower) — deliberately un-Keynesian message[^heilbroner-018].
- Shared bourgeois admiration but opposite futures: Keynes — intrinsically threatened stagnation, optimism requires government support; Schumpeter — intrinsically dynamic, no permanent spending needed (though permissible to alleviate distress), short-run climbing trajectory where a century is a "short run," final judgment negative on survival[^heilbroner-018].

# The man — aristocratic airs, elite loyalty

- Joseph Alois Schumpeter (1883–1950), same birth year as Keynes, father died age 4, mother remarried distinguished general age 11, sent to Theresianum — exclusive aristocratic sons' school — acquiring manners, riding habit at faculty meetings, and tastes that clung lifelong; lifelong boast of three wishes — great lover, great horseman, great economist — "alas, life had granted him only two"[^heilbroner-018].
- Vienna star student — "never a beginner" (Spiethof) — yet enfant terrible openly disagreeing with Eugen von Böhm-Bawerk; sojourn in England, brief unhappy marriage; Cairo financial adviser to Egyptian princess — halved rents while doubling her income by taking no more than legally entitled personal income; published first book on economic theorizing there, professorship in Austria, then *The Theory of Economic Development* (1912, age 27) instantly a small masterpiece[^heilbroner-018].
- Career after theory: 1919 commission on nationalization for socialist German government — to young economist's challenge: "If somebody wants to commit suicide, it is a good thing if a doctor is present"; 1919 finance minister in center-socialist Austria — ambitious stabilization plan resigned before approval, would likely have failed amid inflation; president of Biedermann private bank Vienna — failure via storm and associates' dishonesty, personally in debt, paid creditors in full behind bankruptcy laws costing capital, repaid over ten years[^heilbroner-018].
- Personal losses — charming 21-year-old daughter of mother's apartment superintendent, loved five years, married, died in childbirth within a year; had concealed her humble background — claimed French/Swiss schooling while she worked as maid in Paris; thereafter visiting professor Japan then Germany then Harvard, married economist Elizabeth Boody, campus figure with cloak and *douche* remark[^heilbroner-018].

# *The Theory of Economic Development* — circular flow, profit, entrepreneur

## A capitalism without accumulation

- Paradoxical opening: a book about capitalist growth begins with growth totally absent — a changeless "circular flow" reproducing itself never altering, stripped of capital accumulation — the ingredient that gave Smith/Mill/Marx/Keynes their growth; resembles Ricardo-Mill stationary state but for them it was the end, for Schumpeter the setting for the beginning[^heilbroner-018].
- Inertia as rule: "All knowledge and habit, once acquired, becomes as firmly rooted in ourselves as a railway embankment in the earth" — trial-and-error optimal course repeated by routine; challenge becomes habit[^heilbroner-018].
- Competition removes all earnings exceeding contribution: workers paid full value of product, landowners rents for resource contribution, capitalists receive nothing except wages of management — any value from owned capital goods absorbed by labor and resource values embodied — "in a static economy there is no place for profit!" — as Ricardo/Mill foresaw[^heilbroner-018].

## Where profits come from

- Profits vexed — Smith wavered between deduction from labor value (implies labor shortchanged) and independent return in capital itself (why to owner not inventor/user); Mill "abstinence" reward unexplained; others paid "capital" as if shovel paid; Marx: Smith right first time — deduction, but labor theory discredited[^heilbroner-018].
- Schumpeter's answer: profits do not arise from exploitation nor earnings of capital but when circular flow fails routinized course — introduction of technological or organizational innovations (cheaper ways or wholly new goods) creates income flow traceable to neither labor nor resource — like differential rent of favorably located land but springing from will and intelligence of innovator, disappearing as others learn trick — wholly transient profit[^heilbroner-018].

## Entrepreneur as revolutionist, not profit receiver

- Innovation implies innovator — not normal routine businessman but member of another group (not class — innovators come from any social class) called *entrepreneur* — old word reused for revolutionists of production, source of profit but not necessarily receiver (profit goes to enterprise owner like rent to landowner; entrepreneur squeezed out by dynamics he set in motion); even more than Ricardo's capitalist, Schumpeter's entrepreneur denied share[^heilbroner-018].
- Not profession or heritable position but special leadership — less socially esteemed than generals/statesmen, perceiving/seizing business advantage; affective traits of other leadership absent, precarious position, when raised socially no cultural tradition, upstart laughed at — "this type has never been popular"[^heilbroner-018].
- Motives: "First, there is the dream and the will to found a private kingdom, usually, although not necessarily, also a dynasty.... Then there is the will to conquer: the impulse to fight, to prove oneself superior to others, to succeed for the sake, not of the fruits of success, but of success itself.... Finally, there is the joy of creating, of getting things done, or simply of exercising one's energy and imagination." — romantic knight errant, Veblen workmanship plus predatory drive, not Smith public esteem nor Marx forced accumulation, not bourgeois by necessity but aspiring to become one, breathing life into tame merchantdom (Mann's *Buddenbrooks*)[^heilbroner-018].

## Beyond profit — interest, credit, cycles

- More than paean: theory of interest, credit, and business cycle — leadership rare, followership easy; on heels of innovator comes *swarm* of imitators generalizing improvement, bank borrowing and investment spending creates boom; generalization removes differential advantage, competition forces price to new cost, profits vanish as routine takes over, investment contracts, ill-timed investments turn sour — cycle mechanism to revisit[^heilbroner-018].

# The Depression test — *Business Cycles* (1939)

- Depression challenged entrepreneurial energy story: Keynes could invoke low "animal spirits" without explaining why; Schumpeter had to explain why innovations failed to arrive on time via bunching/swarming[^heilbroner-018].
- Two-volume 1000-page *Business Cycles* (1939): (1) not one but three concurrent cycles — short (Kitchin), 7–11 year (Juglar), 50-year epochal (Kondratiev) tied to steam locomotive/automobile — all touching bottom together; (2) external negative factors — Russian Revolution to inept government policy — outside theory yet gravitating severity; swarming as cause never well established[^heilbroner-018].
- Deeper thesis: capitalism needs faith beyond bread — faith in bourgeois civilization it produces and reproduces — and despite economic success that faith was losing mobilizing force; book ends contradictorily — next-to-last sentence predicts next three decades much more buoyant if three-cycle schema correct, last sentence: "But the sociological drift cannot be expected to change" — hint of 1942 vision[^heilbroner-018].

# *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy* (1942) — plausible capitalism, sociological failure

## Marx as worthy opponent

- Keynes immediate bête noire — philosophical opposition plus personal irk at Keynes's world attention vs Schumpeter's academic recognition; Schumpeter's *General Theory* review with bowings ("one of the most brilliant men...") but dismissal ("the less said about [it] the better") — uncomprehending unbecoming; real antagonist Marx — student seminar with Hilferding and Bauer, more familiar than any Western economist then (much Marx untranslated until 1950s), more open-minded about Marx than Keynes at Harvard[^heilbroner-018].
- Book opens Marx the Prophet/Sociologist/Economist/Teacher — agrees capitalism is dialectical change and self-created disequilibrium, immanent development source for Schumpeter, but diverges on cause: Marx class struggle squeezing surplus motivates all capitalists to labor-saving innovation; Schumpeter bourgeois side — "evolution of the bourgeois style... genesis of the lounge suit" (Veblen-worthy) — thrust not from swashbuckling warrior but bourgeois rational hedonist's opposite, and not from bourgeois capitalist at all but outsider interloper entrepreneur; other differences aside, Marx formidable intellect to be met on own ground[^heilbroner-018].

## Can capitalism survive? No — but not for Marx's reasons

- Tour de force "plausible capitalism" — scenario mirroring Keynes's century growth: stagnationist vanishing investment opportunities dismissed with airy wave — conquest of air as great as India; monopoly sclerosis dismissed — innovation as "perennial gale of creative destruction" whose agents *are* monopolies — continuous self-renewing growth model directly refuting Marx economically[^heilbroner-018].
- Then contradiction: economically successful but sociologically failure — economic base creates ideological superstructure — rational not romantic, critical not heroic, lounge suits not armor — rationalist critical frame after destroying other institutions turns against itself: "Capitalism creates a critical frame of mind which, after having destroyed the moral authority of so many other institutions, in the end turns against its own; the bourgeois finds to his amazement that the rationalist attitude does not stop at the credentials of kings and popes but goes on to attack private property and the whole scheme of bourgeois values."[^heilbroner-018].
- Entrepreneurship ends as atmosphere changes — personality/character count less, bureaucratic management more, innovation institutionalized to routine, bourgeois family transmission belt infected with rationalism, bourgeois loses faith in itself; surface well while "there is a tendency toward another civilization that slowly works deep down below."[^heilbroner-018].
- "Can socialism work? Of course it can." — Schumpeterian benign bureaucratic planned economy; beaten Marx on own ground by conceding survival answer yet substituting own reasons — capitalism yields to socialism not via proletarian rise or deepening crises but cultural ennui[^heilbroner-018].

# Heilbroner's appraisal — dazzling but uneven, not an economic prognosis

- First impression dazzled admiration mixed irritation — attitudinizing tweaking conservatives and Marxist zealots, pet ideas: Marx as great conservative, monopolies increase sphere of better brains, more capitalist nation less aggressive (British imperialism / U.S. foreign policy counterexamples)[^heilbroner-018].
- Prescience if published 1942: wide-open technological frontier, bureaucratization, waning bourgeois ethic — putting to shame Left's expectant demise, middle's modest fiscal fix, Right's road-to-serfdom forebodings[^heilbroner-018].
- On close exam less impressive: right about frontier but not quality dangers — nuclear arms/energy to computerization pose dangers as well as investment fields; right about big-business bureaucracy but wrong that giants decline in aggressiveness — vast multinationals contending for world markets contradict dwindling expansion drive[^heilbroner-018].
- Ennui prediction: persuasive late-1960s as Western capitalism seemed moving to planning, but by thirty years later unconvincing — U.S. and Europe revival of capitalist belief after planning produced growth then inflation then loss of faith, Soviet collapse coup de grâce; Heilbroner notes critique is within short run while Schumpeter wrote long run — revival may be short-lived, drift to mildly socialistic capitalism may resume, multinationals may cartel into imperial kingdoms — but these are speculations[^heilbroner-018].
- Core methodological point: not ultimately economic prognosis but shrewd social-political assertions not predictably lawlike as Smith/Marx accumulation or competitive merchant — disaffected intellectual spoiling outlook and businessman deciding game not worth candle obey cultural not economic imperatives; "processes of economics are not sufficient in themselves to determine how the system goes" — cannot be judged by same criteria as classical seers; aristocratic aloof scholarly stance plus hard politics/enterprise experience may have placed Schumpeter better than Keynes (too easy success) or Marx (none) to judge drift, but at cost of strict economic logic[^heilbroner-018].
- Disquieting implication for economics itself — great achievement of worldly philosophers was deducing societal direction; if Schumpeter right, predictive capability no longer matters — question reserved for final chapter[^heilbroner-018].

# Elites, hotels, and the Carusos — history as elite dynamics

- Juxtaposition static circular flow vs dynamic gale as clue: development not intrinsic to capitalism as such but dynamization by noncapitalist elite! Schumpeter believer in elites — minorities with unusual gifts[^heilbroner-018].
- Musical analogy: every healthy man can sing; half average, quarter diminishing, quarter above average, within that quarter diminishing numbers with increasing ability until Carusos — so with leadership — Schumpeter estimates quarter deficient consigned to clerks/functionaries, next half normal innovating capacity — "practically all business people" relying on comfortable ruts yet adaptable to daily challenges, then true elite "people who are a type characterised by supernormal qualities of intellect and will."[^heilbroner-018].
- History as elite impact on inert mass — military talent feudal, economic talent market, but driving minority always there; "The upper strata of society are like hotels which are indeed always full of people, but people who are forever changing."[^heilbroner-018].
- Thrust at Marxian proletarian revolution: wrong because sheer numbers place proletariat in normal range; individual proletarians may have leadership but leadership only tiny minority[^heilbroner-018].
- Why philosophical about socialism's advent: managerial economy needs possessors of ability — bourgeoisie "Here is a class, which, by virtue of the selective process of which it is the result, harbors human material of a supernormal quality and hence it is a national asset which it is rational for any social organization to use." — skills to run socialism like those to run advanced capitalism, so elite finds natural position at top — not conventional economics but historical sociology: not classes but elites seize commanding heights; economics describes results in market-rewarding societies, but always Carusos run show[^heilbroner-018].
- Social vision fleshing out economic model; journalistic term "vision" central to Schumpeter's posthumous *History of Economic Analysis* (working at death 1950) — analysis glory but preceded by preanalytic process inescapably colored by values: "Analytic work ... embodies in the picture of things as we see them, and wherever there is any possible motive for wishing to see them in a given rather than another light, the way in which we see things can hardly be distinguished from the way in which we wish to see them."[^heilbroner-018].

# Vision and what we wish to see — Marshall's missed boat

- Illustration Schumpeter likely unaware: Marshall's *Principles* discussing consumers' goods vs producers' goods notes "a distinction to which some prominence has been given" — Keynes's crucial consumption vs investment insight just around corner — but Marshall calls it "vague and perhaps of not much practical use" because his vision emphasizes pricing process not consequences for future growth; from pricing perspective right — no fundamental difference pricing shirts vs machines — missed Keynesian boat; had eyes focused on total output like Keynes he would have seen it[^heilbroner-018].
- Asks whether economics analyzes what we wish to see rather than detached objective world — to be weighed next chapter with worldly philosophers' accomplishments[^heilbroner-018].
- Final knot — Theresianum aristocratic values transferred to history — elite as aristocracy belief in natural superiority of chosen few core to aristocratic society, but chosen not by blood but by "intellect and will" — aristocracy of talent — Schumpeter's own group, justifying not mere name/birth but durable worth; congruence of personal experience and historic vision unravels contradictions; Schumpeter would not likely welcome assessment but not deny[^heilbroner-018].
- Closing personal note — never lectured on own theories despite entreaties; scholar suggests felt formulations inadequate; unclear whether wished-for greatness as economist denied, but as visionary certainly great; as analyst or visionary everyone interested in economics must come to grips with him, not only for disciplinary accomplishment but for demonstrating its limitations[^heilbroner-018].

# Contradictions

- Keynes vs Schumpeter on nature and future of capitalism: Keynes intrinsically stagnation-prone requiring government support yet long-run optimistic (2030 plenty); Schumpeter intrinsically dynamic needing no permanent prop yet long-run pessimistic sociologically ("Can capitalism survive? No") — Heilbroner presents as diametrically differing prognoses despite shared bourgeois values[^heilbroner-018].
- Circular flow vs gale of creative destruction: Schumpeter depicts capitalism simultaneously as static changeless habit-bound reproduction without profit and as continuous self-created transformation — Heilbroner notes methodological purpose (isolating innovation rent) and deeper interpretation as noncapitalist elite dynamizing inert mass[^heilbroner-018].
- Entrepreneur generates profit but not necessarily receives it; upstart despised yet system savior; seeks bourgeois kingdom yet system erodes bourgeois family and faith — unresolved social tensions in Schumpeter's account[^heilbroner-018].
- Heilbroner's appraisal vs Schumpeter's 1942 claims: 1960s planning drift seemed to vindicate sociological surrender; 1980s–90s market revival, continuing multinational aggressiveness, and technology dangers seemed to falsify — Heilbroner leaves as speculation not law, judged as social not economic prognosis[^heilbroner-018].
