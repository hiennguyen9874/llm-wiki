Before roughly the sixteenth to seventeenth century, Heilbroner argues, a separate self-contained economic world had not lifted itself from its social context, and the idea that society could be bound by a general scramble for monetary gain was viewed as blasphemous or mad[^heilbroner-002].

# Synthesis

The pre-modern economy was *embedded* in political, religious and kinship life; markets existed as local exchanges but not as a *market system* sustaining the whole society, because the abstract agents land, labor and capital did not yet exist as impersonal, saleable entities and gain itself lacked legitimacy[^heilbroner-002].

# The illegitimacy and rarity of gain

Gain diffused throughout society is modern, not timeless:

*   **Middle ages:** Church taught no Christian ought to be a merchant; merchants seen as disturbing yeast[^heilbroner-002].
*   **Shakespeare's time:** ordinary citizens sought to *maintain* station, not advance it; only gentility could advance[^heilbroner-002].
*   **Pilgrim fathers:** gain as tolerable goal was doctrine of the devil — illustrated by Boston 1639 trial of Robert Keayne, fined £200 for >6d profit on the shilling; minister thereafter condemned three "false principles of trade": selling as dear / buying as cheap as possible, raising prices on remaining goods after loss at sea, and reselling at purchase price though overpaid[^heilbroner-002].
*   **Wage evidence:** Sir William Petty (cabin boy, hawker, clothier, physician, professor of music, founder of Political Arithmetick) observed labor scarce when wages were good because workers labored "only to eat, or rather to drink" — still seen among unindustrialized peoples who take time off rather than work harder for higher wages[^heilbroner-002].
*   **Wealth without generalization:** Phoenicians, Roman speculators, Venetians, Hanseatic League, Portuguese/Spanish voyagers, and the Fuggers (gold/silver mines, right to coin money, credit greater than kings) show wealth of the few, not a society-wide venture spirit. At Anton Fugger's height his nephews Hans Jacob, George and Christopher each refused the banking empire to live in peace[^heilbroner-002].
*   Even enterprising exceptions like Saint Godric of Finchale (beachcomber → merchant → hermit) were rare in a world where earthly life was preamble to Life Eternal[^heilbroner-002].

# Markets are not the market system

Markets as exchanges are ancient — Tell-el-Amarna tablets c.1400 B.C. (Gold/chariots for slaves/horses), medieval traveling fairs, and varied primitive trades — but their existence does not imply a market *system*[^heilbroner-002].

Illustrative cultural contrasts: among New Zealand Maoris asking how much food a bonito hook is worth is ridiculous; in some African communities asking how many oxen a woman is worth is legitimate — the latter narrowed where dowries persist[^heilbroner-002].

The market system is a *mechanism for sustaining an entire society*, not merely exchanging goods. Medieval minds could not envisage it because its components — abstract Land, Labor, Capital — had not been conceived[^heilbroner-002].

# Why the components did not exist

Land, labor and capital as soil, humans and tools are ancient; as abstract, dehumanized, saleable agents they are as modern as the calculus[^heilbroner-002]:

*   **Land:** As late as the fourteenth–fifteenth century there was no freely saleable rent-producing property. Estates/manors/principalities were foundations of prestige, military, judicial and administrative life, not real estate. A medieval nobleman would no more sell land than a modern governor would sell counties. Sales occurred only under heavy strings[^heilbroner-002].
*   **Labor:** No labor market of individuals selling services to highest bidder. Rural serfs baked at lord's oven, ground at lord's mill, tilled fields and served in war as duty, rarely for pay, tied to estate; urban apprentices/journeymen had terms, numbers, pay, hours and methods fixed by guild, bargaining only in sporadic strikes — no more a labor market than hospital interns[^heilbroner-002].
*   **Capital:** Private wealth existed but without aggressive use; motto was safety-first. Longest, most labor-consuming technique preferred; advertising forbidden; quality improvement treated as treason. In sixteenth-century England a "wonderworkshop" of ~200 looms with butchers/bakers for staff was outlawed by the king as dangerous efficiency and concentration of wealth[^heilbroner-002].

Without these abstractions, society ran by local command and tradition — lords' orders and custom explained everything. Had Adam Smith lived before 1400 he could have been a moral philosopher but never an economist; there was nothing for him to do[^heilbroner-002].

# Vignettes of an embedded world

Heilbroner uses time-travel stops to show gain's marginal role and the economy's embeddedness[^heilbroner-002]:

*   **France 1305 fair:** merchants with armed guards, striped tents, Levant/Scandinavia goods (divan, syrup, tariff, artichoke, spinach, jar from Araby), business books as notebooks ("Owed ten gulden by a man since Whitsuntide. I forgot his name"), Roman numerals, errors, zero poorly understood; tiny volumes — Saint Gothard pass annual goods wouldn't fill one modern freight train, Venetian fleet not one steel freighter.
*   **Germany c.1550 Andreas Ryff:** bearded fur-coated Baden merchant, 30 markets, saddle burn; toll ~every 10 miles, 31 levies Basle→Cologne; around Baden alone 112 length, 92 square, 65 dry, 163 cereal, 123 liquid, 63 liquor, 80 pound measures; each community own money/rules/law.
*   **France/England regulation:** Merchant Adventurers Company articles forbidding indecent language, quarrels, card playing, hunting dogs, unsightly bundles — more lodge than firm; Colbert 1666 règlement fixing Dijon/Selangey fabrics at 1,408 threads (including selvages), Auxerre/Avallon/etc. 1,376, Châtillon 1,216; cloth pilloried on first two offences, merchant pilloried on third.

Common point: propriety of gain unaccepted, economic life not yet separated from social life; work was an end in itself as tradition, not a means to money[^heilbroner-002].
