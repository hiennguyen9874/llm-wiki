Heilbroner presents Adam Smith not as apologist for business but as the first to systematize how self-interest and competition self-regulate provision and drive a secular ascent through division of labor, capital accumulation and population response — a bounded, consumer-centered optimism later misread as laissez-faire dogma[^heilbroner-004][^heilbroner-005].

# Synthesis

The chapter contrasts the brutal, haphazard England of the 1760s — Cornish tinners lounging half the day by candle- and pipe-breaks, Durham/Northumberland pits where stripped men, women and half-starved children dragged tubs and pregnant women gave birth underground, roving Welsh harvest bands unwaged except sixpence a day, and the 1742 Lombe silk mill with 26,586 wheels driving 73,726 yards per water-wheel revolution tended by children on 12–14 hour shifts — with Smith's claim to discover order beneath chaos: a System of Perfect (Natural) Liberty guided by an invisible hand[^heilbroner-004].

# The man

*   Self-description "I am a beau in nothing but my books"; medallion profile — protruding lower lip, aquiline nose, heavy bulging eyes — lifelong nervous head-shake and stumbling speech; vermicular gait, eyes on infinity, lips moving, hesitating step; indulgent absentmindedness — falling into a tanning pit while discoursing, brewing bread-and-butter as tea, walking 15 miles in a dressing gown, imitating a saluting guard's pike-drill up a stair[^heilbroner-004].
*   Born Kirkcaldy, Fife 1723 (pop. 1,500, nails still used as money), father dead before birth; kidnapped age four by gypsies, abandoned after uncle's pursuit — "He would have made, I fear, a poor gypsy"[^heilbroner-004].
*   Glasgow scholarship to Oxford at 17 on horseback, six years largely untutored — professors given up teaching, fellows reading novels in debate; nearly expelled for Hume's *Treatise of Human Nature*[^heilbroner-004].
*   Chair of Logic 1751 at 27 and soon Chair of Moral Philosophy at Glasgow — centre of the Scottish Enlightenment. Accused of smiling in church, friendship with Hume, dispensing with Sunday Christian evidences, petitioning to dispense with opening prayers, "natural religion"; teacher Francis Hutcheson had broken ground by lecturing in English not Latin. Dean 1758, loved by students — even Boswell came, busts in booksellers' windows, lecturing despite levity charges[^heilbroner-004].
*   *Theory of Moral Sentiments* (1759) — how self-interested man forms impartial moral judgments by occupying a third-person observer's sympathy; instant sensation, German *das Adam Smith Problem*[^heilbroner-004].
*   Patron Charles Townshend — Horace Walpole's wit "must have been greatest man of his age, if only he had common sincerity, steadiness and sense," quip pain in his side without specifying which side, Chancellor whose judge-election refusal and tea duty precipitated American Revolution — married Countess of Dalkeith (Duke of Buccleuch's widow); offered Smith £500 p.a. plus expenses and £500 life pension to tutor the duke (vs ≤£170 professorial fees). Pupils refused refund[^heilbroner-004].
*   Grand Tour 1764–66: 18 months Toulouse (boring company, execrable French) beginning *Wealth of Nations*; south of France (met Voltaire, repulsed amorous marquise), Geneva, Paris[^heilbroner-004].
*   Paris encounter with François Quesnay, physician to Louis XV and Mme. Pompadour, Physiocracy's *tableau économique* — wealth flows like blood, not gold/silver hoard; Mirabeau elder ranked it with writing and money; advocated laissez-faire but called manufacturing "sterile" because only agriculture added with Nature's help. Smith, impressed enough to have planned dedication, rejected the bias — Scottish commerce showed labor itself creates value[^heilbroner-004].
*   Tour cut short 1766 by death in delirium of duke's younger brother despite Quesnay's aid; retreat to London then Kirkcaldy for ~10 years dictating against fireplace leaving pomade streak, visits to Dalkeith, London literary circle — Samuel Johnson club clash "You lie!" / "You are a son of a—!"; Benjamin Franklin supplied American facts and conviction that colonies would become "one of the greatest and most formidable that ever was in the world."[^heilbroner-004]
*   *Wealth of Nations* published 1776; Commissioner of Customs, Edinburgh 1778 (£600 sinecure); lived with mother to her 90; died 1790 aged 67, buried Canongate churchyard "author of *The Wealth of Nations*"; curiously little notice amid French Revolution; honors — Burke visit, Lord Rector Glasgow, translations Danish/French/German/Italian/Spanish, never Oxford honorary; Pitt, Addington, Wilberforce, Grenville rose when he entered: "we will stand until you are first seated, for we are all your scholars"[^heilbroner-004][^heilbroner-005].

# The book

*   Not strictly original — draws on Locke, Steuart, Mandeville, Petty, Cantillon, Turgot, Quesnay, Hume (>100 authors named) — but first wide-net synthesis; "outpouring not only of a great mind, but of a whole epoch"[^heilbroner-004].
*   Panorama from pin manufacture to herring catch since 1771 to American disturbances and Oxford student waste; Cannan index 63 fine-print pages touching all: e.g. Abassides through Butcher, brutal and odious business; 900 pages, meandering — 75-page digression on silver, sociology of morality chapter — yet aphoristic: "nation of shopkeepers," philosopher vs street porter vs mastiff/greyhound, East India Company "very singular government ... though the whole country was swallowed up by an earthquake"[^heilbroner-004].
*   Not a textbook: polemic against live Mercantilism (200+ pages to kill) — wealth is not gold/money but the flow of goods and services consumed by all; therefore democratic/radical though retaining poverty; aimed at empire governance, not classroom[^heilbroner-004].
*   Revolutionary in implication though Smith distrusted bourgeois ("mean rapacity," "neither are, nor ought to be, the rulers of mankind") and admired work while suspecting motives; sided with consumer: "Consumption is the sole end and purpose of all production"[^heilbroner-004][^heilbroner-005].

# The vision: two problems

Smith's blueprint — called Political Economy / today economics — asks (1) how centrifugal self-interest is held together without planner or tradition, and (2) whither society moves over time. The answer to (1), the market, becomes engine of (2)[^heilbroner-004].

## 1. Laws of the market — the invisible hand

> "the private interests and passions of men" led "which is most agreeable to the interest of the whole society"[^heilbroner-004]
> "It is not from the benevolence of the butcher, the brewer, or the baker that we expect our dinner, but from their regard to their self-interest. We address ourselves, not to their humanity, but to their self-love, and never talk to them of our necessities, but of their advantages."[^heilbroner-004]

*   **Impulse:** self-interest drives men to whatever work society will pay for.
*   **Regulator:** competition — flock of similarly motivated actors eager to exploit greed — prevents holding society to ransom, transmutes selfish motives into social harmony as in *Moral Sentiments*[^heilbroner-004].

Mechanism illustrated:

*   **Prices:** 100 glove makers each wish to raise price above cost; attempted rise is undersold; collusion would be broken by entrant from shoemaking shifting capital to steal market[^heilbroner-004].
*   **Quantities:** consumers want more gloves, fewer shoes → glove prices/profits rise, shoe prices/profits slump → workers released from shoes to gloves → production rises/falls back to demand, prices normalize; no dictum needed[^heilbroner-004].
*   **Incomes:** unduly high profits/wages attract rush until equalized at skill/training levels; too low causes exodus until adjusted; thus market explains price-to-cost discipline, adaptation to demand, self-cure of high prices, and rough equality of incomes within strata — a self-regulating, anonymous taskmaster where appeal is impossible and freedom's price for violating it is ruination[^heilbroner-004].

Historical fit: 18th-century England approximated atomistic competition — small factories, prices moving with demand; deviations already visible — manufacturer combinations, journeymen associations, coming large-scale industry (Lombe factory), unequal power of children as market agents — foreshadowing modern weakening by giant corporations and unions, yet underlying self-interest/competition still operative[^heilbroner-004].

## 2. Laws of motion — accumulation and population

More than static equilibrium, *Wealth* is a history of how natural liberty came to be and where it tends — with market as both cohesion and motion[^heilbroner-004].

**Productivity base:**

*   Division of labor — pin factory: one draws, one straightens, one cuts, one points, one grinds, two–three head it, one puts on, one whitens, one papers; ten poor men poorly equipped make ~12 pounds/day (~48,000 pins), vs perhaps 20 or one per man separately — simplification multiplying output and "universal opulence ... to the lowest ranks"[^heilbroner-004][^heilbroner-005].
*   Woollen coat passage — shepherd, sorter, comber, dyer, scribbler, spinner, weaver, fuller, dresser, merchants, carriers, shipbuilders, sailors, sail-makers, rope makers etc.: "exceeds all computation" — thousands cooperate so that "meanest person in civilized country" is better accommodated than an African king absolute over 10,000 naked savages; exemplifies gain obscured by late-18th-century squalor but real versus century before[^heilbroner-005].

**Law of Accumulation:** rising capitalists — Arkwright barber-apprentice to £500k at death 1792, Walker forge to £200k steel foundry Rotherham, Wedgwood wooden-leg potter to £240k + land — hoard to accumulate, not charity (Manchester £2,500 Sunday-school collection, cotton spinners £90). Smith approves if invested in machinery to deepen division, not for its own sake; avarice again serves community — but assumes indefinite investibility limited only by geography; denounces encroachment on capital: "like him who perverts the revenues of some pious foundation to profane purposes, he pays the wages of idleness with those funds which the frugality of his forefathers had, as it were, consecrated to the maintenance of industry"[^heilbroner-005].

Accumulation raises demand for hands → wages → profit squeeze threatens.

**Law of Population:** labor supply expands with demand, like any commodity: "the demand for men, like that for any other commodity, necessarily regulates the production of men." Mechanism is not birth rate alone but infant survival — Highlands mother of 20 with two alive, half of English children dead before four, almost half only to nine–ten; higher wages reduce malnutrition/disease deaths, swelling worker numbers who then compete wages down, rescuing accumulation — aggravation and cure in one automatic reciprocation[^heilbroner-005].

**Giant endless chain:** market equalizes returns → accumulation → greater division/production + wage rise → wage pressure on profits → population increase → wage pressure reversed → accumulation resumes. Not a business cycle but secular evolution, tastes and physical resources alone outside cause-effect[^heilbroner-005].

**Bounds and stationary state:** Smith sees division as once-for-all advance, not self-generating continuous innovation; growth can continue until unused resources exhausted and division fully exploited — Smith mentions ~200 years as longest flourishing — then wages revert to subsistence, capitalists to modest stable profits, landlords alone may gain from sustained food demand. Labour improves for decades, but very long-run optimism is sober[^heilbroner-005].

# Politics: laissez-faire misread

*   *Wealth* took eight years to be quoted in Parliament (Fox admitting unread), nine English editions by 1800 plus Europe/America; champions were rising capitalists seizing "let the market alone" despite Smith's consumer bias[^heilbroner-005].
*   Smith favours minimal, not zero, government. Warns mass-production stultifies — "understandings ... generally becomes as stupid and ignorant as it is possible for a human creature to become" unless government prevents — and assigns three duties: (1) defence against violence/invasion, (2) exact administration of justice, (3) public institutions/works "which may be in the highest degree advantageous to a great society" but private profit cannot repay — roads, education (today flood control, ecological repair, research implicit)[^heilbroner-005].
*   Enemy is monopoly/interference that impedes fluid market — Smith: "People of the same trade seldom meet together, but the conversation ends in a conspiracy against the public, or in some diversion to raise prices" — e.g., hatter ≤2 apprentices, Sheffield cutler ≤1, pauper parish settlement blocking mobility, chartered monopolies denying cheaper foreign produce. All meddling lowers welfare; later humanitarian laws (whitewashing factories, forbidding shackling children to machines) were opposed by quoting Smith out of context[^heilbroner-005].
*   Applied with full faith — judges paid by litigants to expedite cases, joint-stock companies doubted for lack of personal self-interest, slavery's abolition defended as cheaper — reducing complex irrationality to polarity toward profit/away from loss, social magnetism akin to physics where removing barriers is man's task[^heilbroner-005].

# What survived?

*   Not the bounded evolution — profoundly altered by successors — but the market panorama as foundational analysis of commercial capitalism's expansive phase before business cycles, giant firms, unions or corporate forms matured. Smith missed the Industrial Revolution as disruptive quality change, positing quantitative growth within static quality — dynamics of a static community growing but not maturing[^heilbroner-005].
*   Durable anticipations — Veblen 150 years early on parade of riches, statesmanship on colonies ("If any provinces ... cannot be made to contribute... it is surely time that Great Britain should free herself..."); together with *Moral Sentiments* reveals philosopher-psychologist-historian-sociologist envisioning human adventure under Great Architect of Nature, optimistic without utopianism, serene criticism without rancour[^heilbroner-005].

> "No society can surely be flourishing and happy, of which by far the greater part of the numbers are poor and miserable"[^heilbroner-005]

# Contradictions

None within Smith's chapter, but Heilbroner notes tension between Smith's intent and appropriation: Smith the cautious consumer advocate vs. capitalists' "economic patron saint" justifying blockage of factory and child-labour regulation by selective quotation of laissez-faire[^heilbroner-005].
